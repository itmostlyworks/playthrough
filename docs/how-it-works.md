# How Playthrough works

Everything Playthrough does, in detail. To start, read the [README](../README.md).

Its parts:

- **`packages/review`**: what a review is, and the store that keeps it. A review is rounds the agent records and sends people make, each with one writer. The store takes the loop's steps (start, save a round, take the notes, finish, send, accept, wait) and refuses anything that breaks a rule, saying what's wrong in plain words. It keeps reviews in memory or in a folder. Several people can review, and each person's judgement is their own: what's new to you, which notes wait for you, and whether a flow is good for you are worked out for each person, never kept.
- **`packages/page`**: what's on a page, as a person would name it, and the same thing found again. A reading is the page a step left, everything on it named in Playthrough's fixed words; two readings of the same step on two rounds pair thing by thing, which is how the comparison finds what changed, where an answer lands, and where a note sits in every round.
- **`packages/cli`**: the commands an agent runs, over the store, with reviews kept in `.playthrough/` in the app's repo; run from a folder inside the app, a command finds them by looking up from there, as far as the top of its git repo. Each of git's worktrees is a copy of the app with reviews of its own, kept beside the work they're about, so an agent in a worktree never writes outside it; a review's name is the same in every copy, so starting one refuses a name another worktree has. A word more than a command takes is refused, never ignored, and `--help` anywhere is the help. It records each flow, reads the page each step leaves, compares each round with every round before it, walks the person's steps in the app again for each note made there, places each answer where its note's thing is on the new round, and pictures each note made on a recording for the agent. `playthrough open`, from any copy, serves every copy's reviews, each recorded, walked and taken over in the copy it's kept in, and once one is open for the app, says its address again rather than opening another. It serves the viewer to this machine alone, and answers only the viewer itself: asked by any name but localhost's, as a site that has made its own name lead here (DNS rebinding) would ask, or sent something from another site's page, it refuses, as what's sent there reaches the agent in the person's name.
- **`packages/viewer`**: the page a person reviews in: the recording, an action bar across its bottom edge (Note, Compare, Take over, and this flow's notes), and under it the timeline, the speed and the size. A round's flows play like chapters, each opening with its name over the page its first step left, softened, the page its picture shows, a glow of white behind the words, deepest under them and gone at its edges, so the name reads over whatever the page has there (Kan's own centred “Join workspace”) and over a light page the glow isn't seen, the same on the page with every flow, once the review has more than one person in it who does it (“as the owner, then a customer”, as its row on the page with every flow says too), and the agent's line about it under the agent's name, where playing waits the first time you come to it; a click plays and pauses, as a video's does; the recording fits, or shows at actual size as a window that follows the pointer or stays where you drag it, and a flow handed to someone on another screen takes that screen's shape as their page comes, as from one flow to the next, and on a touch screen the recording's pointer is a fingertip, there from the moment their page shows; the timeline answers the pointer and the drag; and the line over the frame says which round you're looking back at, or that the next one is in; one that comes in while you're on the newest is followed once you go to another flow or to every flow, never under the one you're watching, and what was playing plays on. What's new to you since you last looked at a flow is a diamond on the timeline where it starts, new steps too; stopped on one, what changed is outlined on the recording, and new steps on what they click or type into. What the agent changed for a note in its own flow is in the note's answer instead: its dot is the one mark for both. The notes button lists this flow's notes, rising from the bar, a line each, lit on the timeline as you point at it, and last the whole flow, with its square and how it stands. Every mark covers what it shows. Opened, from the timeline, its pin or the list alike, a dot goes still on the frame its note was written on, and a diamond on the frame its change is seen on, new steps on the first step the flow didn't take before; a bar, a note whose answer moves, plays exactly what it covers (the step its reply names, from a second before the first action that set what it's on moving, nothing stopping it on the way), then its card asks. What's open sits beside its spot, covering neither what it's on nor that thing in the round beside it, and can be dragged anywhere and closed with its ×, fading where it stood with the line to its pin, and grows or shrinks to what it says as that changes (Edit, Not quite, earlier answers) rather than jumping; a note just saved drops its dot onto the timeline as its pin dropped onto the recording, and the speed and size roll over as the round does when you change them. A note's card is the conversation: what was said, what the agent changed for it under its answer (pointing at one outlines just it, as in a change's card), and “Does this fix it?” with Compare beside it (Looks good plays on; a saved Not quite stays open, paused, your words in it, until Enter plays on); what changed isn't put in Playthrough's words: the recording marks it, each change on its own with a diamond pin of its own, in the page's own ink, light over a dark page and dark over a light one, a fine line that only strengthens for the change you're on, its diamond filling, nothing growing or moving, around what's seen of its thing (a heading is its words, not the page's width it's laid out across; a card, a field or a button, what it draws), and a change inside another's box drawn only by its pin until you're on it, words reworded marked themselves (“on their own”, not the sentence they were added to), and what's gone left to Compare, which shows it where it was. Playing stops at each moment with a change new to you, the first time it gets there, and marks them, opening nothing: you look first. Pointing at a change's pin opens its card beside it, only its why, in the agent's words under its name, or the note it was made for, and Note on this, and it goes a moment after the pointer leaves the pin and the card; pointed at, it never moves the view, which carried the pin out from under the pointer and back again; pressed, the pin keeps it open, chosen and brought into view as anything chosen is, until it's pressed again, and another's pin opens that one instead. The card stays as long as its marks do, just past its moment too. A scroll, kept every few hundredths of a second, plays eased between them, at the screen's own rate. Compare (C), held, shows this moment as it was, in place, on the round you last looked at the flow on or else Before (for an answer, the round its note was written or sent back on); pressed, the two play together side by side, or one above the other, whichever shows each larger, until it's pressed again or Escape. On a bar, held or pressed, the two play its stretch side by side, again and again, until you let go or press it again, and it rests at the stretch's end. The flow as a whole is a mark like a note's: the timeline ends in its square, so you know where playing leads and how it stands: amber while it asks you, with ↑ when that's sending what's yours in it, green and ticked once it looks good, slate and ticked once it's with the agent; pressed, from the list's last row (“The whole flow”) or with Up and Down too, it goes straight to the end, which counts as watching it, and opens the flow's card there; reaching the end, however you get there, always brings it up. The end is a place, as the start is: the last frame softens under the veil the flow's name has at its start, and the card stands in the middle of it, the same square at its head, popping with the timeline's when you answer. In it is each note on the whole flow, laid out as its conversation (what was said, the agent's answer under its name, and what it changed, each with its time, which goes there: the playhead stops on it, outlined in the note's colour and lit on the timeline, and playing on comes back to the end), yours editable in place and one being written among them, then the one question under them all, whether it's good: Looks good, which settles the answers in it you've seen, those laid out over the end among them (one you haven't, elsewhere in the flow, is shown first) and is sent as you say it, a flow's end being where what you said in it goes; it stays a checkbox, pressed again to take it back, which is sent too, and the flow asks again, its tick landing there and the next step offered under it, or with nothing left to do, All flows, so the end always has a step (Next opens the next flow as choosing it from every flow does, its name up, then playing; one with something of yours to send opens at its end); or Not quite, another note on the whole flow, which leaves the answers laid out as they were and sends without them. With something of yours in it, it asks to send that instead: Send, so the agent starts on it while you watch the rest; sent, it's with the agent, and the step is what's next. Enter takes the one step from where the playhead is (Play on, Next) and Ctrl+Enter accepts. A note you start and leave with words in it is kept, not saved. So is a Not quite or an Edit you leave with new words in it, until the page closes: opening the note again finds the box as you left it, the cursor at the end, and Save or Cancel ends it. What you haven't sent and the flows you've watched are kept in your browser for the review, each under the round it's about, and show on later rounds as what you sent does: a Looks good holds and a watched flow (one whose end you've reached, however you got there) stays watched while the flow is unchanged, and a note waits to be sent at its flow's end; Send carries each round's part as a send on that round. The round is the page with every flow, even one, where you arrive: under the work's title what was asked, in the person's words, then the round, then a row for each flow (a small picture, its name and length, and where it stands, in words), the frame beside them showing the one you point at, and under it the one step, Start checking, Carry on checking or Accept, or looking back, Back to now (carrying on to a flow with a note to send, or one you didn't save, opens it there, as Next does, not from its start), each flow's row with its end's square beside where it stands; choosing a flow grows its picture into the frame, and All flows over the recording, Escape (from a flow's end too, in one press), the end's All flows or accepting brings you back, the frame shrinking home into that picture, which shows the flow you left until you point at another. The address names the flow you're in, so reloading finds you at its start. Without a review's address, or from Playthrough's name at the top of one, you arrive at every review of the app, from every worktree, laid out as a round is a level up: the app's name, then a row for each review (its first picture, its title, where it stands for you in its round's words, as “Round 8 · 4 flows to check”, “Round 9 coming”, “Accepted” or “Nothing recorded yet”, and when it last moved), what waits for you first and otherwise the newest, the frame beside them showing the one you point at, its title over its first picture, where the round's frame will be once it opens. Pressing the round and moving sideways goes back through the rounds, every picture flipping at once, as the prototype's label did; once you've sent something that asks the agent for an answer (a plain Looks good asks for nothing), it says the next round is coming, its dot breathing, and when it's in. With Note on (N), what a click would note is outlined, named as Playthrough names everything (“Settle up button”, “$600.00”, the “Who owes whom” area), and Alt with the wheel widens it to what holds it; a click on empty space is named by what's near it. A note sits on its thing in every round, and one on empty space beside the thing it's named by, as far from it as it was, so a note beside a card that moved down a list moves down with it, its pin just outside the outline; playing is watching, so while anything plays, everything drawn over the recording fades (cards, pins, outlines, the line between them, and the timeline's words; the bar stays, so what you can do is there while you watch), and comes back where it was once it stops, the card kept by its pin while the playhead is still on its mark (once it has left a dot's moment, a diamond's or a bar's stretch, played on or dragged, the card is closed), where it stands until the recordings' layout changes (Compare on or off, the window resized) and it glides to a place clear of what it's on and, where it can, of what changed, the side it opened on first; beside it, the earlier round outlines the same thing, for a change too; an open note's answer is outlined in its colour wherever it changed, every place of alike things changed alike, on both rounds, except on the note's own thing or what holds it, which is one thing, outlined by the note alone, as it is on every round, its card still clear of the change, which is drawn while you point at it, on the round beside it around where that thing was; at actual size the view brings what you chose into view, and beside it the earlier round shows where that thing was, so a thing that moved is in view on both; and a note's card greys when what it's on leaves the page. Your note on a change grows out of the change's card: your words under its name, its colour a dot on the diamond, and opened, Compare under your words. On a phone the page runs down the screen: the recording at the top as large as the width lets it be, its controls under it, and every flow at once is its list, then the frame, scrolled as one.
- **`example`**: a small invoices app to try it on.

On npm it's one package, `playthrough`, which an app installs as a dev dependency so its flows can import `playthrough/test`: what `playthrough` prints tells the agent to add it the first time, with Playwright's Chromium, so a person only asks for the change, and a run in an app without it says to install it. The repo is a skill too, `skills/playthrough/SKILL.md`, which says only when to reach for Playthrough and to run `npx playthrough`, so what the agent reads stays in one place, matched to the version the app has: `npx skills add itmostlyworks/playthrough` gives it to most coding agents, and in Claude Code the repo is a plugin as well, from its own marketplace (`.claude-plugin/`). `npm run build` makes it in `packages/cli/release` (`packages/cli/build.ts`): Node runs TypeScript from a checkout but not from inside an installed package, so there the command is JavaScript, with the review and the page inside it, beside the viewer it serves, the types flows are written against and what it prints. Every push to main is checked, tested and built (`check.yml`), so main says whether it's ready long before a release. It's released from GitHub: in the Actions tab, Release, Run workflow, with the version. That sets the version, the package's and the plugin's, checks, tests and builds it, tries it installed in an empty app, publishes it as a trusted publisher (no token kept anywhere), and tags the version on main with a release. It releases main as it is: a run re-run later, on the commit it started on, stops before anything, saying to start a new one. The command's file is named without “./”, which npm would otherwise take out as it publishes.

## The loop

```bash
playthrough start <review> "<title>" "<what was asked>"   # a title for the change, as its pull request's, and the person's words
playthrough start <review>             # once its flows are written: Before, the app as it is
playthrough record <review>            # records every flow, the way a person would use it
playthrough wait <review>              # hands the round over, then waits for the notes to answer
playthrough walk <review>              # walks the person's steps in the app again, on the app as it is now
playthrough open [<review>]            # the viewer, on this machine
```

1. The agent starts a review and writes its flows in `.playthrough/<review>/flows/` before it changes anything. They show what was asked, so they're written from the task, not the code. Each flow is one Playwright test for each thing a person does, with a `test.step` for each part of it, importing `test` and `expect` from `playthrough/test`. The test's name is the flow's. `playthrough` alone prints everything an agent needs to know, once (`packages/cli/INSTRUCTIONS.md`); each command then says what's next.
2. `playthrough start <review>` again tries the app's setup (4), then keeps Before: the app as it is, as a commit, with whatever wasn't committed yet. Nothing's recorded: the flows are written for the work, and each is recorded on Before with its round (5). Outside git, or in a repo with no commits yet, where git can't set the work aside to put the app as it was back, there's no Before.
3. The agent changes the app. `playthrough record` records the app as `playthrough/app.json` starts it. Where the command can be given a port, `{port}` in both (`{ "start": "npm run dev -- --port {port}", "url": "http://localhost:{port}" }`), Playthrough starts this copy on a free port of its own, the same for as long as it runs, so a worktree's copy runs beside the person's own and beside any other's. At the address the app always has (`{ "start": "<command>", "url": "<address>" }`), it records on whatever serves it and starts the app only when nothing does (a server still at a request after two seconds, as a dev server building a page is, serves it), except in a worktree, where what answers could be another copy: there it says the address must be free, and that a port in `app.json` ends the question; a copy it started from another worktree gives the address way. In a folder, one command runs the app at a time, as a dev server like Next's runs only one there and what's recorded has to be the work as it is now: the app the viewer keeps ready for taking over is stopped when any other command starts the app, and started again, on the work as it is then, the next time the viewer needs it; while another command runs it, taking over waits, saying to try again once it's done. Whose turn it is is kept in `.playthrough/turn.json`, each command known by its process on the same start of the machine. Either way it starts the app itself rather than through Playwright, so the app goes when Playthrough does, whatever stops it, and one it started is started again on the app as it was when flows are recorded on Before; it runs each flow with rrweb in every page, the pointer gliding to what it acts on and a beat after each action, and what's filled into a text box, a text area or an editor typed a key at a time (60 ms apart, a long text in 1.5 s at most; a date or a number is set as it is), and keeps each recording with where its steps start. It records a quarter as many flows at once as the machine has cores, the longest by last round first, so a round ends close to when its longest flow does (five flows on Kan: 126 s one at a time, 50 s four at once); an app with a page that shows what every run made, as a blog's index lists every post, says `"oneAtATime": true` in `app.json`, and each flow waits for the one before it. Beside each other, flows run up to a tenth slower, and a message the page announces and takes away by itself, a toast (`aria-live`, or a status or an alert), may be read just after it's gone: so a message is judged by whether the step showed it, in what was glimpsed after each action or just after the step, not by whether it's still up when the step is read, and it's called a message, the same one whatever page it pops up over. A dialog isn't a message: one that now closes is gone. The browser's own dialogs, an alert, a confirm or a prompt, aren't in the page, so rrweb never sees them: each is kept in the recording as a moment of its own, with what it says, held long enough to read (1.2 s, and 40 ms a character, up to 4 s), then answered as the flow answers it, or No, as Playwright would; the action that opened it waits for the answer, however long it was held. The viewer draws it as the browser does, at the top of the window under where the page came from, in the same look in both themes, its answer pressed just before it goes. A page the setup left loaded, where the flow starts, is taken whole as the recording starts: before, a flow that didn't load a page itself played blank until it did. Each step ends once the page is at rest (nothing it asked for on its way, and nothing on it changing or moving for 300 ms of the frames it draws, on its own clock: a page too busy to draw is working, not still), and the page it left is read there as the app shows it, not as the pointer hovers it: everything a person would name, with its box and its look, and a picture. After each action, it's glimpsed as well, read without a picture (a hundredth of a second or two, after the beat), so a note can be on what comes and goes inside a step, like a menu or a dialog; the comparison and a note's place on other rounds go by the steps alone. A flow that fails records nothing, and `record` names it and the step it failed at, and keeps the page it stopped on in `.playthrough/<review>/failed/`, as Playwright wrote it (`error-context.md` and a picture), until a run has none failed; where Before stops is how far the app as it was went, not a mistake, and nothing of it is kept. While flows record, each says how it went in a line as it ends (to its end, and how long; where it stopped, or failed), and a sentence about more than three of them says how many rather than naming each, as fifteen names ran to a paragraph, and the reasons come after, in Playwright's words, where a wait that ran out says what it waited for, a locator that found more than one thing names the first two as Playwright would find each alone, and what an action waited for in its place, as the pointer glides there, fails as that action (`locator.click`), not as the wait it is: Playwright's own list ticked a flow whose checks Before let go by and printed a stack for one that stopped where it should. An action waits up to 10 s for what it acts on, and on Before 3 s, as a flow that shows what's new stops at the first thing the app as it was doesn't have. Recording again runs the flows that failed first, and stops there if one fails again; only once they pass does it run every flow. The fonts, images and stylesheets each page loads are kept with its recording, once each by what's in them, and the viewer plays them from there, so a recording looks the same with the app gone or on another machine; it plays light or dark, still or moving, as its browser was, each page saying so as it's taken, its styles and the SVGs it draws answered that way whatever the viewer's browser says (`page/src/scheme.ts`, as the app taken over is answered too), where a site recorded light played dark in a dark viewer and turned light as it was taken over; of the sizes an image offers, it shows the one it was recorded at.
4. Every flow starts from the same place each round. Playthrough runs it on the day the review started, at the time of day it's recorded, the clock running on from there, in English and UTC, with the page's random numbers from a fixed seed: what the page works out from the day reads the same each round, and what the app's server stamps with the real time reads as it would (“less than a minute ago”), every round that day. The rest is the app's, in `playthrough/setup.ts`, written once and committed beside `app.json`, and loaded as a module, as flows are, whatever the app's own package.json says: its default export runs before each flow, given the page, Playwright's request and who the flow is done as, and what it returns is the flow's `setup` (the group it opens, say). Its `use`, if it exports one, is how every page of the app is opened, as Playwright's `test.use` would, under what a flow's file says: a flow, a page it's handed to, the setup's trial and a run taken over, so an app that serves tests its own way, told by a user agent as Actual's demo budget is, gets it everywhere; what Playthrough keeps the same for every flow, or a flow sets with `test.use` (the screen, the language and time zone, light or dark, motion), it refuses, naming it. Every flow is done by someone the app knows: setup names the app's people, as the team says them, each with a line in the team's words for the agent, and a flow names who it's done as with `test.use({ person })`, beside its screen (`phone` and `tablet` come with `test`), or it's done as the first; setup's `signIn`, if it exports one, signs a page in as them. A flow can be handed on: `handTo(person, screen)` gives someone else a page of their own in the same world, signed in, on their screen, and the recording carries on there, each step after it saying who did it, the page left held a moment and the new one a moment before anything's done on it, so the hand-off can be followed, so what lands on their page is what the first person really did. Each run makes its own, and gives it back once it's over: `setup.ts`'s `after`, if it exports one, is given a request of its own and what setup returned, and takes it away, as a flow's recording ends, a run following the playhead stops, or, for the app taken over, as Playthrough ends. Until then it's written down in `.playthrough/borrowed/`, under the process it was made for, so what a stopped Playthrough left, the next command gives back, and an `after` that failed, it tries again whole, so `after` takes what's gone already as given back; so a shared app isn't left with a world for every run. The first time flows run in an app, and whenever `app.json` or `setup.ts` changes, the setup is tried by itself before anything's recorded: the app started as `app.json` says, or the one running used (a command that ends before the app answers says so at once, with the last it said, its stack trace's frames left out, as it does when the app doesn't answer in three minutes), then what a flow starts from made and signed in as each of the app's people, twice, given back each time, so a setup that works once and not again (an account that exists already) is refused in a line rather than as every flow failing. It prints how long each took, the address each was left at, and the error the app answered it with, if it did (a 404 at an app's `/` that has no page there), with a picture of it in `.playthrough/tried/`, and what's missing with what it costs the person, only where it's seen: no `after` where setup made something (sent the app a request that changes what it holds, from its page or its own request, or returned what it made), and what each run makes stays in the app; over two seconds, and each flow and each moment taken over waits that long. A setup that only opens a page, or an app that keeps everything in the browser, costs nothing, and isn't told as a cost. What flows leave behind is said once round 1 is recorded, as it's seen there: a flow that sends the app, on any port of its host, what changes what it holds, with no `after` to give it back or no `setup.ts` at all, is named with the first such request. An `after` that failed in the trial is said to be the setup's to fix, not a cost to tell the person. Nothing's recorded that time: the command ends there, so the agent looks at each picture and fixes what it shows before anything starts from it, then runs it again. It tells the person how flows get into their app, and asks before changing it for Playthrough's sake, without waiting for the answer. What was tried is kept by the two files' contents, so it's tried again only once they change. Until the round it was first recorded in is handed over, a flow is recorded twice each time, on the same app, and what differs between the two changes by itself, like a time the server stamps: `record` says what it is, and it's left out of every comparison, in every flow it's seen in. Learned on the work, it covers what the work added too. Two recordings that don't go the same way (one fails where the other didn't) are refused: the flow doesn't start from the same place each time.
5. Before is for the person: this moment as it was, beside how it is now, so the difference is the change, and what the agent changed on the way shows. So each flow is recorded on it with the round it's first recorded in, once the round's flows have all passed, doing what the round does, as far as the app as it was takes it. What a flow expects of the finished work only waits a moment there, and a flow stops where the app as it was can't do a step, kept up to the step before. Each action the round did is noted by what it was done on, by its role and name as `getByRole` finds it, and on Before, an action that would be done on something else stops the flow there too: a flow is written for the work, and in the app as it was what it looks for can find another thing (the header's last button, Paid where the work put Export; a card where Kan's list menu is), and what it did there isn't how the app was. The same thing is the same role with most of the shorter name's words in the other's, so a card the work gave a badge is still that card. Nobody is asked to judge where Before stopped: the agent's flows are checked on the round, where they have to pass. Until its round is handed over, a flow's Before follows it: recorded again, in place of what it had, once its file, `playthrough/` or what the round does has changed (`.playthrough/<review>/ran-on-before.json` keeps a hash of the three), and gone from Before once the flow is gone. Once handed over, it's been compared, and Before keeps it. Recording on Before happens in the agent's own working copy, where its env files and packages are: the work is set aside with git stash, Before is checked out with `playthrough/` as it is now, since that's how flows get into the app and goes with them, not with the work, and the work is put back after, even when recording fails. The repo's git hooks don't run for any of it, as none of it is anyone's work, and a hook that fails or installs packages on every checkout would stop Before or slow it. Meanwhile every other command that runs the app or keeps it as the work, and taking over from the viewer, is refused, saying to try again once it's done, as the working copy holds the app as it was; one stopped hard is known by its process, on the same start of the machine, so a number another process has after a restart is never taken for it. It's recorded on whatever serves the app, as every round is, so a dev server already running serves the app as it was for that minute, and is given a moment to catch up with the work again before the round is compared, and a Take over meanwhile gets it too. Stopped halfway, Playthrough puts it back before it ends, or the next command does, or it says the two git commands that do. A flow the app as it was can't start has no Before. A flow the browser stopped, not the app (a page that crashed), says nothing about the app: the round isn't recorded, and recording again tries again.
6. Then `record` compares the round with Before and with every round before it, flow by flow, steps matched by title. A change is a thing and what happened to it, in Playthrough's own words, never the agent's: “Export button is new, and moved the Show choice left”, “Status column looks different” where only the pictures differ (a thing that was read moved only if the reading says so: what the picture shows moving on it, as Next's dev indicator over Kan's “Robin Park”, is it looking different, and only what's moved where nothing's read is said to have moved), “New steps: “Export them”” where a flow takes steps it didn't. The same change in several flows is one change, and in a flow it's seen where the recording first has it on the screen, as the person watching meets it, not where the page first has it: a page read top to bottom showed all twelve of its changes at its first step, below the screen as most were, and now shows them a screen at a time (one the flow never brings into view keeps the first step it's there). Words that kept most of what they said, in order, their start and end two thirds of what they were, are those words reworded wherever they went, and what's marked is what reads differently: the site's close, a line about the video added and moved down the page by a new section above it, is that line, where it was a paragraph gone and another new, three boxes; a paragraph rewritten keeps too little of itself, and is one gone and another new. One thing moved straight up or down on its own, by something above it that took room or gave it, is part of that change, as many moved alike are. A control whose words changed is that control reworded, even a little wider and moved for it (“Clear completed button now says “Clear 2 completed””), not one gone and one new; a list whose items are in a new order is told as the fewest of them that moved, of those in it both times, the rest having only made room: two that traded places have swapped, called by their words, one or two that went past others moved that many places (“STU-9 Print the programme 7th Nov” moved down 2 places, in Kan's sorted list, where it was said to have swapped with one of the two it passed), and more are a new order; other things that traded places, each as many things strong, have swapped too; and words whose box is only narrower or wider, with no background or border to show it, still starting or ending where they did, aren't a change: words that did move on the screen, the pictures find. A person reads a row, a list's item or a card as one thing, so the page is read with its items: what the page says is a row or a list's item, and anything one of several side by side with the same tag and class, as Actual's payees are. Items pair across the rounds by what's the same in both inside them, then, in lists side by side, in order when there are as many; words only in the same place say nothing of which is which. An item says what's read in it, not a menu's options it keeps hidden. A whole item new or gone is one change, called by its words past an avatar's initial (“Maya maya-…@kan-test.local Member” is gone, not its name, its email, its role and its ⋯ each); an item whose words changed in more than one piece is one change saying what it says now (“Apples -- 12 votes” now says “Apples 57% 12 votes”); how something in it looks, or where it is, is told on its own. Inside an item, a thing is known by its place there, its column and which of the things of its kind in it it is, in which kind of item of which list, words reworded by how they changed too, and a move by which way it went, each word with a number in it left out as data; never by its own words, which in an item are its data. So the same change in each item is one change: the polls app's new shares were fourteen changes and are one, in 3 places; Actual's twenty-three changes are four, its new transaction counts one of them: “96”, “2”, “2” and 17 more are new, and moved 20 things left; and “(paid)” after each customer's name is one change, in 6 places, where it was a table with six things changed inside. Six customers each renamed differently are still six changes, counted. A change seen in several flows is told in the one that joined the review first, not the one whose file sorts first, and a flow recorded on Before again keeps its place there; every round keeps its flows in that order too, the task's first and one new to the round last, where they went by their files' names and the issue's own flow came last. What changes by itself is kept as the changes it stands for, each by itself, and left out as that, before anything is told together: Kan's members each with a new email every run made the members table one change of eight things, which left the whole table out of every comparison; now it's each email. A part of a picture that changes by itself, known only by where it is, with nothing read there or only near it, is left out only in its own flow: the same place in another flow is another thing, and Kan's invite page lost a real change to a patch that changed by itself on the members page. `record` lists each change since the round before, by its id.
7. The agent says why each of them is there in the round's file, `.playthrough/<review>/rounds/<n>/round.json`, under `accounts` by the change's id: its line, the id of the note it was made for, or why nothing really changed. Each that Playthrough can name, that is: one only the pictures show, with nothing read there changed (“Robin Park looks different”, under Next's dev indicator), is the person's to look at, as they see what it is at a glance and the agent can't see more of it than Playthrough did; it's outlined on the recording as every change is, and `record` tells the agent of it in a line that asks nothing, unless it's what the agent changed for a note, which it can still say, so it shows in the answer (the sample's “Message field looks different”). Read in any flow, a change is one Playthrough can name. It answers each note there too, where `record` left a reply for each with empty words, placed where the note's thing is on the new recording: found by pairing what was read at the same step, never said by the agent. A note made in the app is answered where the person's steps lead: `record` runs the flow to where they took it over, does their steps again after it, recorded, and places the reply where they got to; where their steps stop short, it says where, and the agent fixes what the step is on or says why in its answer. A flow that's new to the reviewer can have a `line` at its start: what it starts from, or the choice it shows. Recording again keeps what it wrote.
8. Each round says which agent recorded it. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Gemini CLI are known from their shell; any other agent writes its name in the round as `by`, and `record` asks it to until it does.
9. `playthrough wait` hands the round over, which the rules allow once every note has an answer and every change since the round before has its account; if one hasn't, it says which. Handed over, it says to tell the person it's theirs to review, then, as the notes come, where their answers go. `record` says how to word an account, and to hand the round over in the background, in a line each, only the first time the review is recorded; after that, what's still to do says what's missing, rather than the same paragraph every round. Then it waits for a send that asks for something and prints each note to answer, with its conversation so far, whose each verdict is, and a picture of the page as the person saw it, what the note is on outlined, or for a note on the whole flow, the page the flow ended on; a reply missing its words says it's the reply's “words”, under “replies”. A note made in the app, taken over, has no picture: `wait` says what the person did there instead. It names the review and where its flows are, for an agent new to it.
10. In the viewer, each person watches, leaves notes, says Looks good or Not quite to each answer and Looks good to each flow, and sends. A note has one state for everyone, the last word on its answer, whoever said it and under their name: Looks good settles it, and a Not quite, even after someone's Looks good, sends it back to the agent. An answer waits for whoever asked for it, by writing the note or sending it back; anyone can judge it. Looks good on a flow, and accepting, are each person's own. Anyone can accept once every flow is good for them and nothing waits for the agent, and `wait` says the review is done.

## Taking a flow over

```bash
playthrough take-over <review> "<flow>" ["<step>"]
```

In the viewer it's Take over (T), on the action bar across the frame's bottom edge: while the latest round plays, its flow runs alongside in the background, as far as the playhead has gone and no further, so paused, the moment the playhead rests on is ready a beat later and loaded unseen while you look, and Take over turns the frame itself into the app there, at the same size and place, the time saying Live. It's where you paused, to the action, or the flow's start while its name is up: a recording keeps when each of a step's actions was done, the flow runs as far as the recording had got, and what the flow's actions changed only in the page's memory, like a search typed or a form opened, in this step or the ones before it, is done again in your browser before it shows, while one that saved something or went to another address isn't done twice, nor anything before it, as loading the page brings those back, and it's scrolled where the recording was at that moment, and light or dark, still or moving, as recorded, a scroll not being an action it stops at (a page being read, taken over partway down, opened a screen up); the recording's pointer, whose tag names whoever has the flow at that moment, and, handed on as it plays, changes its name as it does here, growing to it, so the hand-off is seen where both pages look alike, comes to yours and gives way to it, the name going with it (“You, as a customer”); Taking over is doing: from Take over until you're back, the app is over all the recording draws, the veil of a flow's start or end too, and what drives the recording rests: the recording itself, where a click or a pin does nothing; All flows over it and the row under it (the timeline, which still says Live and grows your fork, speed and size), stepping back to a third in the app; an open card about it, gone in the app, where it would cover what you're doing, and back as it was when you are (one about the app, a note made there or being tried, still answers); and the keys with them, so only the app, Note and Back answer, and a note chosen from the bar goes back to the recording at it. Escape, in the app or out of it, lays the recording back over it, so what was open there stays open, and T again carries on where you were; but it's the app's first, as N is while you type: if the app does something with it, closing a menu, a list or a dialog, that's all it does; in a field you're typing in, it leaves the field; and the next Escape, which it lets be, goes back. A run can't go back, so going back before it starts another. One app is made ready for each review at a time: making the next one ready, at the next pause, gives back the one before, taken over or not, and its address goes with it, so a session of pauses leaves two worlds in the app at most, the one ready and the run following the playhead. A flow whose file says it reaches outside the app (a Playwright annotation on the flow or a `describe()` around it, `{ type: "reaches outside", description: "sends Sam an email" }`) is never run ahead: not alongside the playhead, nor as it rests, only when it's taken over, as every pause would otherwise send that email again. The runs are Playthrough's own: each is a window of its own, the one its flow is recorded in (a phone's screen and touch, say, or another language, when the flow's file says so with `test.use`, in the file or a `describe()`), with its own cookies and storage, in one headless browser Playthrough keeps open while it runs, so a new one opens in a moment and nothing is kept waiting; following Kan costs one Chrome, about 350 MB, where a Playwright run of its own for each, with a spare, held about a gigabyte. Recording still runs the flows as Playwright tests. Asked for before it's ready, the recording's pointer says Taking over… and, once a run taken over has got there before, counts down to it, “Taking over in 3…”, a second at a time: each run taken over keeps how long it took from each moment to the next, not counting waiting for the playhead, which on the same app is the same each time to within a few percent, and how long it took to hand the page over once it got there, and the viewer adds the quickest the app has loaded in the frame, as one slow load, a page the app builds the first time, isn't every load; a run following the playhead isn't counted, as it keeps everything to do again and its page settles while it waits. If the app isn't there when the count is done, the pointer says Taking over… until it is. A run told where it will be taken over goes straight through the steps before it, not waiting for the playhead, so a moment deep in Kan's longest flow is ready in about four seconds, not nine. From the command line, it runs the flow to the end of that step (or of the whole flow) on the app as it is now, quickly and unrecorded, from what every flow starts from, then hands its page to you at http://localhost:5830/take-over/ and a name made up for that moment, which no one could guess: the same address, cookies and what the app kept in the browser, so you carry on by hand in the real app, from a fresh copy of that moment. The app is passed on through Playthrough while the command runs, on every network this machine is on, so a phone on the same one can open it too, but only that phone: opening the moment's address from elsewhere hands a pass with the page, which the app never sees, and anyone else there is turned away. Taken over in the viewer, the app is passed on to this machine alone. Either way it answers only to this machine's names and addresses, so a site that has made its name lead here gets nothing, and only a page served from this machine, as the viewer is, may read what the app sends through it, as a recording made there asks the app for its fonts and pictures again. On Spliit, with the app already running, it's ready in about three seconds; on Kan too, where the setup signs a new user up through Kan's own sign-in, so the session is Kan's real one, a cookie the page can't read, and it carries over with nothing special for Kan. The page runs on the recordings' clock, so times on it read as they did there. Only the app as it is now can be taken over, not Before.

## Downloading a flow as a video

In the viewer it's Download video (V), at the right of the line the review's title is on, there in a flow and gone on every flow at once: the flow you're on, as it's shown, alone or side by side with the round Compare shows, as an MP4 your browser downloads, named for the flow and its rounds (“Send a reminder · Round 7 and Round 8”). It's for a pull request, a teammate, a post: something that plays anywhere, with nothing to install. `playthrough open` films it in a browser of its own (`packages/cli/src/video.ts`): the viewer plays the flow there with nothing but its recordings (`packages/viewer/src/Video.tsx`), opening on its name over the softened page as in the viewer, and closing on its last frame softened the same way, with the logo and playthrough.sh where the name stood. Chromium films a page only at its screen's size, so the page draws itself at the size the video is, no wider than 1920 and no taller than 1920, never more than twice its own size; drawn no larger, Chromium hands over a frame every 60th of a second the page changes, so it's filmed as it plays, and each frame goes into the video as it's filmed, at 60 frames a second, as smooth as a screen shows it, in HD video's own colours (BT.709, in the range a TV shows, which every player takes a video to be: the browser's own conversion kept a page's full range, and players that took it as a TV's blew the contrast out), with that browser's own H.264 (`packages/cli/src/mp4.ts` writes the file). So it takes about as long as the video, a second more: 16 seconds for “Send a reminder”, ten seconds of flow with its name and its end.

Making it, the button says so where you pressed it, as taking over does (`packages/viewer/src/DownloadVideo.tsx`): “Downloading in 14…”, counting down a second at a time with only the number changing, filling from the left as it goes, and ending on a tick, “Downloaded”, as your browser takes the file, then back. `open` sends what it has left as it's made, a line at a time, then the video: the playing still to go, and the frames waiting to go in.

## Working on Playthrough

`npm test` holds the review model, the comparison, the command line and what the viewer shows each person to their promises, in about a minute and a half. The comparison's rules are each tested on two small pages, and the command line is run for real in Chromium, as two stories side by side, each in a copy of the example made a git repo. While iterating, `npm run quick` runs everything but those two stories, in about ten seconds; run `npm test` before each commit. `npm run check` checks the types. While working on the viewer, `npm run dev --workspace @playthrough/viewer` serves it with instant reloads beside a running `playthrough open`.

Needs Node 24 or later, which runs the TypeScript as it is.
