# How Playthrough works

Everything Playthrough does, in detail. To start, read the [README](../README.md).

Its parts:

- **`packages/review`**: what a review is, and the store that keeps it. A review is rounds the agent records and sends people make, each with one writer. The store takes the loop's steps (start, save a round, take the notes, finish, send, accept, wait) and refuses anything that breaks a rule, saying what's wrong in plain words. It keeps reviews in memory or in a folder. Several people can review, and each person's judgement is their own: what's new to you, which notes wait for you, and whether a scene is good for you are worked out for each person, never kept.
- **`packages/page`**: what's on a page, as a person would name it, and the same thing found again. A reading is the page a step left, everything on it named in Playthrough's fixed words; two readings of the same step on two rounds pair thing by thing, which is how the comparison finds what changed, where an answer lands, and where a note sits in every round.
- **`packages/cli`**: the commands an agent runs, over the store, with reviews kept in `.playthrough/` in the app's repo; run from a folder inside the app, a command finds them by looking up from there, as far as the top of its git repo. Each of git's worktrees is a copy of the app with reviews of its own, kept beside the work they're about, so an agent in a worktree never writes outside it; a review's name is the same in every copy, so starting one refuses a name another worktree has. A word more than a command takes is refused, never ignored, and `--help` anywhere is the help. It records each scene, reads the page each step leaves, compares each round with every round before it, runs the agent's fork of each note made in the app, places each answer where its note's thing is on the new round, and pictures each note made on a recording for the agent. `playthrough open`, from any copy, serves every copy's reviews, each recorded, its forks run and taken over in the copy it's kept in, and once one is open for the app, says its address again rather than opening another. It serves the viewer to this machine alone, and answers only the viewer itself: asked by any name but localhost's, as a site that has made its own name lead here (DNS rebinding) would ask, or sent something from another site's page, it refuses, as what's sent there reaches the agent in the person's name.
- **`packages/viewer`**: where a person reviews. A review opens on its page, below (The page); what follows is the player a scene plays in: the recording, an action bar across its bottom edge (Note, Compare, Take over, and this scene's notes), and under it the timeline, the speed and the size. A round's scenes play like chapters, each opening with its name over the page its first step left, softened, the page its picture shows, a glow of white behind the words, deepest under them and gone at its edges, so the name reads over whatever the page has there (Kan's own centred “Join workspace”) and over a light page the glow isn't seen, the same on the page with every scene, once the review has more than one person in it who does it (“as the owner, then a customer”, as its row on the page with every scene says too), and the agent's line about it under the agent's name, where playing waits the first time you come to it; a click plays and pauses, as a video's does; the recording fits, or shows at actual size as a window that follows the pointer or stays where you drag it, and a scene handed to someone on another screen takes that screen's shape as their page comes, as from one scene to the next, and on a touch screen the recording's pointer is a fingertip, there from the moment their page shows; the timeline answers the pointer and the drag; and the line over the frame says which round you're looking back at, or that the next one is in; one that comes in while you're on the newest is followed once you go to another scene or to every scene, never under the one you're watching, and what was playing plays on. What's new to you since you last looked at a scene is a diamond on the timeline where it starts, new steps too; stopped on one, what changed is outlined on the recording, and new steps on what they click or type into. What the agent changed for a note on a moment in its own scene is in the note's answer instead: its dot is the one mark for both. What it changed for a note on the whole scene is a change like any other, met where it happened, its diamond carrying the note's dot. The notes button lists this scene's notes, rising from the bar, a line each, lit on the timeline as you point at it, and last the whole scene, with its square and how it stands. Every mark covers what it shows. Opened, from the timeline, its pin or the list alike, a dot goes still on the frame its note was written on, and a diamond on the frame its change is seen on, new steps on the first step the scene didn't take before; a bar, a note whose answer moves, plays exactly what it covers (the step its reply names, from a second before the first action that set what it's on moving, nothing stopping it on the way), then its card asks. What's open sits beside its spot, covering neither what it's on nor that thing in the round beside it, and can be dragged anywhere and closed with its ×, fading where it stood with the line to its pin, and grows or shrinks to what it says as that changes (Edit, a reply, earlier answers) rather than jumping; a note just saved drops its dot onto the timeline as its pin dropped onto the recording, and the speed and size roll over as the round does when you change them. A note's card is the conversation: what was said, and the agent's answer under its name, with what it changed for the note marked on the recording in the note's colour, never listed in Playthrough's words, which ran to four lines over the recording that quoted a dialog's text mid-word; and “Does this fix it?” with Compare beside it (Looks good plays on; a saved reply stays open, paused, your words in it, until Enter plays on); what changed isn't put in Playthrough's words: the recording marks it, each change on its own with a diamond pin of its own, in the page's own ink, light over a dark page and dark over a light one, a fine line that only strengthens for the change you're on, its diamond filling, nothing growing or moving, around what's seen of its thing (a heading is its words, not the page's width it's laid out across; a card, a field or a button, what it draws), and a change inside another's box drawn only by its pin until you're on it, words reworded marked themselves (“on their own”, not the sentence they were added to), and what's gone left to Compare, which shows it where it was. Playing stops at each moment with a change new to you, the first time it gets there, and marks them, opening nothing: you look first. Pointing at a change's pin opens its card beside it, only its why, in the agent's words under its name; made for a note, it's that note's answer seen there: the note in a line, as the notes list has it, going to it, where it's judged, then the agent's answer to it as the why, the card's diamond carrying the note's dot as its pin does; and Note on this, and it goes a moment after the pointer leaves the pin and the card; pointed at, it never moves the view, which carried the pin out from under the pointer and back again; pressed, the pin keeps it open, chosen and brought into view as anything chosen is, until it's pressed again, and another's pin opens that one instead. The card stays as long as its marks do, just past its moment too. A scroll, kept every few hundredths of a second, plays eased between them, at the screen's own rate. Compare (C), held, shows this moment as it was, in place, on the round you last looked at the scene on or else Before (for an answer, the round its note was written or sent back on); pressed, the two play together side by side, or one above the other, whichever shows each larger, until it's pressed again or Escape. On a bar, held or pressed, the two play its stretch side by side, again and again, until you let go or press it again, and it rests at the stretch's end. The scene as a whole is a mark like a note's: the timeline ends in its square, so you know where playing leads and how it stands: amber while it asks you, with ↑ when that's sending what's yours in it, green and ticked once it looks good, slate and ticked once it's with the agent; pressed, from the list's last row (“The whole scene”) or with Up and Down too, it goes straight to the end, which counts as watching it, and opens the scene's card there; reaching the end, however you get there, always brings it up. The end is a place, as the start is: the last frame softens under the veil the scene's name has at its start, and the card stands in the middle of it, the same square at its head, popping with the timeline's when you answer. In it is each note on the whole scene, laid out as its conversation (what was said, the agent's answer under its name, and under it each moment it changed something for the note, its diamond and time and no words, going there as the diamond on the timeline does: stopped on it, the change is marked as any change is, its pin opening the note and the agent's answer to it, and playing on plays on), yours editable in place and one being written among them, then the one question under them all, whether it's good: Looks good, which settles the answers in it you've seen, those laid out over the end among them (one you haven't, elsewhere in the scene, is shown first) and is sent as you say it, a scene's end being where what you said in it goes; it stays a checkbox, pressed again to take it back, which is sent too, and the scene asks again, its tick landing there and the next step offered under it, or with nothing left to do, All scenes, so the end always has a step (Next opens the next scene as choosing it from every scene does, its name up, then playing; one with something of yours to send opens at its end); or Note on the whole scene, which leaves the answers laid out as they were and sends without them. With something of yours in it, it asks to send that instead: Send, so the agent starts on it while you watch the rest; sent, it's with the agent, and the step is what's next. Enter takes the one step from where the playhead is (Play on, Next) and Ctrl+Enter accepts. A note you start and leave with words in it is kept, not saved. So is a Not quite or an Edit you leave with new words in it, until the page closes: opening the note again finds the box as you left it, the cursor at the end, and Save or Cancel ends it. What you haven't sent and the scenes you've watched are kept in your browser for the review, each under the round it's about, and show on later rounds as what you sent does: a Looks good holds and a watched scene (one whose end you've reached, however you got there) stays watched while the scene is unchanged, and a note waits to be sent at its scene's end; Send carries each round's part as a send on that round. The round is the page with every scene, even one, where you arrive: under the work's title what was asked, in the person's words, then the round, then a row for each scene (a small picture, its name and length, and where it stands, in words), the frame beside them showing the one you point at, and under it the one step, Start checking, Carry on checking or Accept, or looking back, Back to now (carrying on to a scene with a note to send, or one you didn't save, opens it there, as Next does, not from its start), each scene's row with its end's square beside where it stands; choosing a scene grows its picture into the frame, and All scenes over the recording, Escape (from a scene's end too, in one press), the end's All scenes or accepting brings you back, the frame shrinking home into that picture, which shows the scene you left until you point at another. The address names the scene you're in, so reloading finds you at its start. Without a review's address, or from Playthrough's name at the top of one, you arrive at every review of the app, from every worktree, laid out as a round is a level up: the app's name, then a row for each review (its first picture, its title, where it stands for you as its page says it, never by its round's number, as “4 scenes to check”, “The agent is working”, “All good”, “Accepted” or “Nothing recorded yet”, and when it last moved), what waits for you first and otherwise the newest, the frame beside them showing the one you point at, its title over its first picture, where the round's frame will be once it opens. Pressing the round and moving sideways goes back through the rounds, every picture flipping at once, as the prototype's label did; once you've sent something that asks the agent for an answer (a plain Looks good asks for nothing), it says the next round is coming, its dot breathing, and when it's in. With Note on (N), what a click would note is outlined, named as Playthrough names everything (“Settle up button”, “$600.00”, the “Who owes whom” area), and Alt with the wheel widens it to what holds it; a click on empty space is named by what's near it. A note sits on its thing in every round, and one on empty space beside the thing it's named by, as far from it as it was, so a note beside a card that moved down a list moves down with it, its pin just outside the outline; playing is watching, so while anything plays, everything drawn over the recording fades (cards, pins, outlines, the line between them, and the timeline's words; the bar stays, so what you can do is there while you watch), and comes back where it was once it stops, the card kept by its pin while the playhead is still on its mark (once it has left a dot's moment, a diamond's or a bar's stretch, played on or dragged, the card is closed), where it stands until the recordings' layout changes (Compare on or off, the window resized) and it glides to a place clear of what it's on and, where it can, of what changed, the side it opened on first; beside it, the earlier round outlines the same thing, for a change too; an open note's answer is outlined in its colour wherever it changed, every place of alike things changed alike, on both rounds, except on the note's own thing or what holds it, which is one thing, outlined by the note alone, as it is on every round, its card still clear of the change, which is drawn while you point at it, on the round beside it around where that thing was; at actual size the view brings what you chose into view, and beside it the earlier round shows where that thing was, so a thing that moved is in view on both; and a note's card greys when what it's on leaves the page. Your note on a change grows out of the change's card: your words under its name, its colour a dot on the diamond, and opened, Compare under your words. On a phone the page runs down the screen: the recording at the top as large as the width lets it be, its controls under it, and every scene at once is its list, then the frame, scrolled as one.
- **`example`**: a small invoices app to try it on.

On npm it's one package, `playthrough`, which an app installs as a dev dependency so its scenes can import `playthrough/test`: what `playthrough` prints tells the agent to add it the first time, with Playwright's Chromium, so a person only asks for the change, and a run in an app without it says to install it. The repo is a skill too, `skills/playthrough/SKILL.md`, which says only when to reach for Playthrough and to run `npx playthrough`, so what the agent reads stays in one place, matched to the version the app has: `npx skills add itmostlyworks/playthrough` gives it to most coding agents, and in Claude Code the repo is a plugin as well, from its own marketplace (`.claude-plugin/`). `npm run build` makes it in `packages/cli/release` (`packages/cli/build.ts`): Node runs TypeScript from a checkout but not from inside an installed package, so there the command is JavaScript, with the review and the page inside it, beside the viewer it serves, the types scenes are written against and what it prints. Every push to main is checked, tested and built (`check.yml`), so main says whether it's ready long before a release. It's released from GitHub: in the Actions tab, Release, Run workflow, with the version. That sets the version, the package's and the plugin's, checks, tests and builds it, tries it installed in an empty app, publishes it as a trusted publisher (no token kept anywhere), and tags the version on main with a release. It releases main as it is: a run re-run later, on the commit it started on, stops before anything, saying to start a new one. The command's file is named without “./”, which npm would otherwise take out as it publishes.

## The loop

```bash
playthrough start <review> "<title>" "<what was asked>"   # a title for the change, as its pull request's, and the person's words
playthrough start <review>             # once its scenes are written: Before, the app as it is
playthrough record <review>            # records every scene, the way a person would use it
playthrough wait <review>              # hands the round over, then waits for the notes to answer
playthrough forks <review>             # runs each fork of a note made in the app, on the app as it is now
playthrough open [<review>]            # the viewer, on this machine
```

1. The agent starts a review and writes its scenes in `.playthrough/<review>/scenes/` before it changes anything. They show what was asked, so they're written from the task, not the code. A scene is one situation the change meets: someone, with what they'd really have, there to do one thing, as far as the change reaches, with a step for each part worth looking at; another scene is another scene (someone the change treats differently, things that make it look different, another reason to be there), never the same person, things and way again, as the person watches each from its start. Each is one Playwright test, with a `test.step` for each part, importing `test` and `expect` from `playthrough/test`. The test's name is the scene's. `playthrough` alone prints everything an agent needs to know, once (`packages/cli/INSTRUCTIONS.md`); each command then says what's next.
2. `playthrough start <review>` again tries the app's setup (4), then keeps Before: the app as it is, as a commit, with whatever wasn't committed yet. Nothing's recorded: the scenes are written for the work, and each is recorded on Before with its round (5). Outside git, or in a repo with no commits yet, where git can't set the work aside to put the app as it was back, there's no Before.
3. The agent changes the app. `playthrough record` records the app as `playthrough/app.json` starts it. Where the command can be given a port, `{port}` in both (`{ "start": "npm run dev -- --port {port}", "url": "http://localhost:{port}" }`), Playthrough starts this copy on a free port of its own, the same for as long as it runs, so a worktree's copy runs beside the person's own and beside any other's. At the address the app always has (`{ "start": "<command>", "url": "<address>" }`), it records on whatever serves it and starts the app only when nothing does (a server still at a request after two seconds, as a dev server building a page is, serves it), except in a worktree, where what answers could be another copy: there it says the address must be free, and that a port in `app.json` ends the question; a copy it started from another worktree gives the address way. In a folder, one command runs the app at a time, as a dev server like Next's runs only one there and what's recorded has to be the work as it is now: the app the viewer keeps ready for taking over is stopped when any other command starts the app, and started again, on the work as it is then, the next time the viewer needs it; while another command runs it, taking over waits, saying to try again once it's done. Whose turn it is is kept in `.playthrough/turn.json`, each command known by its process on the same start of the machine. Either way it starts the app itself rather than through Playwright, so the app goes when Playthrough does, whatever stops it, and one it started is started again on the app as it was when scenes are recorded on Before; it runs each scene with rrweb in every page, the pointer gliding to what it acts on and a beat after each action, and what's filled into a text box, a text area or an editor typed a key at a time (60 ms apart, a long text in 1.5 s at most; a date or a number is set as it is), and keeps each recording with where its steps start. It records a quarter as many scenes at once as the machine has cores, the longest by last round first, so a round ends close to when its longest scene does (five scenes on Kan: 126 s one at a time, 50 s four at once); an app with a page that shows what every run made, as a blog's index lists every post, says `"oneAtATime": true` in `app.json`, and each scene waits for the one before it. Beside each other, scenes run up to a tenth slower, and a message the page announces and takes away by itself, a toast (`aria-live`, or a status or an alert), may be read just after it's gone: so a message is judged by whether the step showed it, in what was glimpsed after each action or just after the step, not by whether it's still up when the step is read, and it's called a message, the same one whatever page it pops up over. A dialog isn't a message: one that now closes is gone. The browser's own dialogs, an alert, a confirm or a prompt, aren't in the page, so rrweb never sees them: each is kept in the recording as a moment of its own, with what it says, held long enough to read (1.2 s, and 40 ms a character, up to 4 s), then answered as the scene answers it, or No, as Playwright would; the action that opened it waits for the answer, however long it was held. The viewer draws it as the browser does, at the top of the window under where the page came from, in the same look in both themes, its answer pressed just before it goes. A dropdown's list of options is the browser's too, so the value changed with nothing pressed ("It doesn't even click the drop down, just instantly switches it"): choosing one, the pointer presses the dropdown, its list is kept in the recording as a moment of its own (where it is, its options, the one chosen before), open 0.35 s, then the pointer goes down to the option the scene chooses, and it's chosen there; the page's viewer draws the list as Chrome does, in both themes, the option under the pointer lit, a ring on each press, closing just after the choice. What the page's script animates with `element.animate` isn't in the page's markup either, so rrweb kept a burst of confetti as its pieces sitting still in one speck where they started, then gone ("The confetti looks super weird and small on the replay"): each is written into the page as well, as the CSS animation it is, which the viewer plays as it does the page's own; live, the script's animation is over it and the two end together, and one on an element that has a CSS animation of its own is left alone rather than replace it. A page the setup left loaded, where the scene starts, is taken whole as the recording starts: before, a scene that didn't load a page itself played blank until it did. Each step ends once the page is at rest (nothing it asked for on its way, and nothing on it changing or moving for 300 ms of the frames it draws, on its own clock: a page too busy to draw is working, not still), and the page it left is read there as the app shows it, not as the pointer hovers it: everything a person would name, with its box and its look, and a picture. After each action, it's glimpsed as well, read without a picture (a hundredth of a second or two, after the beat), so a note can be on what comes and goes inside a step, like a menu or a dialog; the comparison and a note's place on other rounds go by the steps alone. A scene that fails records nothing, and `record` names it and the step it failed at, and keeps the page it stopped on in `.playthrough/<review>/failed/`, as Playwright wrote it (`error-context.md` and a picture), until a run has none failed; where Before stops is how far the app as it was went, not a mistake, and nothing of it is kept. While scenes record, each says how it went in a line as it ends (to its end, and how long; where it stopped, or failed), and a sentence about more than three of them says how many rather than naming each, as fifteen names ran to a paragraph, and the reasons come after, in Playwright's words, where a wait that ran out says what it waited for, a locator that found more than one thing names the first two as Playwright would find each alone, and what an action waited for in its place, as the pointer glides there, fails as that action (`locator.click`), not as the wait it is: Playwright's own list ticked a scene whose checks Before let go by and printed a stack for one that stopped where it should. An action waits up to 10 s for what it acts on, and on Before 3 s, as a scene that shows what's new stops at the first thing the app as it was doesn't have. A check stops it there too, after waiting a second: a step is taken on Before only if the app as it was did what the scene expects of it. Checks used to go by, so a phone scene opening a page the app didn't have counted that step as taken, and only the search after it read as new ("isn't the whole thing new?"). Recording again runs the scenes that failed first, and stops there if one fails again; only once they pass does it run every scene. The fonts, images and stylesheets each page loads are kept with its recording, once each by what's in them, and the viewer plays them from there, so a recording looks the same with the app gone or on another machine; it plays light or dark, still or moving, as its browser was, each page saying so as it's taken, its styles and the SVGs it draws answered that way whatever the viewer's browser says (`page/src/scheme.ts`, as the app taken over is answered too), where a site recorded light played dark in a dark viewer and turned light as it was taken over; of the sizes an image offers, it shows the one it was recorded at. Each action's pointer glides to what it acts on, found before the hand sets off, and that's what's acted on, so a button whose name changes as it's pointed at ("Marked done" turning into "Mark as not done") is still the one the scene meant; on a touch screen nothing is pointed at on the way, as a finger comes down where it taps. A scene that stops `playthrough forks` short of where a note's person took it over says where it failed and why, in Playwright's words, as `record` does.
4. Every scene starts from the same place each round. Playthrough runs it on the day the review started, at the time of day it's recorded, the clock running on from there, in English and UTC, with the page's random numbers from a fixed seed: what the page works out from the day reads the same each round, and what the app's server stamps with the real time reads as it would (“less than a minute ago”), every round that day. The rest is the app's, in `playthrough/setup.ts`, written once and committed beside `app.json`, and loaded as a module, as scenes are, whatever the app's own package.json says: its default export runs before each scene, given the page, Playwright's request and who the scene is done as, and what it returns is the scene's `setup` (the group it opens, say). Its `use`, if it exports one, is how every page of the app is opened, as Playwright's `test.use` would, under what a scene's file says: a scene, a page it's handed to, the setup's trial and a run taken over, so an app that serves tests its own way, told by a user agent as Actual's demo budget is, gets it everywhere; what Playthrough keeps the same for every scene, or a scene sets with `test.use` (the screen, the language and time zone, light or dark, motion), it refuses, naming it. Every scene is done by someone the app knows: setup names the app's people, as the team says them, each with a line in the team's words for the agent, and a scene names who it's done as with `test.use({ person })`, beside its screen (`phone` and `tablet` come with `test`), or it's done as the first; setup's `signIn`, if it exports one, signs a page in as them. A scene can be handed on: `handTo(person, screen)` gives someone else a page of their own in the same world, signed in, on their screen, and the recording carries on there, each step after it saying who did it, the page left held a moment and the new one a moment before anything's done on it, so the hand-off can be followed, so what lands on their page is what the first person really did. Each run makes its own, and gives it back once it's over: `setup.ts`'s `after`, if it exports one, is given a request of its own and what setup returned, and takes it away, as a scene's recording ends, a run following the playhead stops, or, for the app taken over, as Playthrough ends. Until then it's written down in `.playthrough/borrowed/`, under the process it was made for, so what a stopped Playthrough left, the next command gives back, and an `after` that failed, it tries again whole, so `after` takes what's gone already as given back; so a shared app isn't left with a world for every run. The first time scenes run in an app, and whenever `app.json` or `setup.ts` changes, the setup is tried by itself before anything's recorded: the app started as `app.json` says, or the one running used (a command that ends before the app answers says so at once, with the last it said, its stack trace's frames left out, as it does when the app doesn't answer in three minutes), then what a scene starts from made and signed in as each of the app's people, twice, given back each time, so a setup that works once and not again (an account that exists already) is refused in a line rather than as every scene failing. It prints how long each took, the address each was left at, and the error the app answered it with, if it did (a 404 at an app's `/` that has no page there), with a picture of it in `.playthrough/tried/`, and what's missing with what it costs the person, only where it's seen: no `after` where setup made something (sent the app a request that changes what it holds, from its page or its own request, or returned what it made), and what each run makes stays in the app; over two seconds, and each scene and each moment taken over waits that long. A setup that only opens a page, or an app that keeps everything in the browser, costs nothing, and isn't told as a cost. What scenes leave behind is said once round 1 is recorded, as it's seen there: a scene that sends the app, on any port of its host, what changes what it holds, with no `after` to give it back or no `setup.ts` at all, is named with the first such request. An `after` that failed in the trial is said to be the setup's to fix, not a cost to tell the person. Nothing's recorded that time: the command ends there, so the agent looks at each picture and fixes what it shows before anything starts from it, then runs it again. It tells the person how scenes get into their app, and asks before changing it for Playthrough's sake, without waiting for the answer. What was tried is kept by the two files' contents, so it's tried again only once they change. Until the round it was first recorded in is handed over, a scene is recorded twice each time, on the same app, and what differs between the two changes by itself, like a time the server stamps: `record` says what it is, and it's left out of every comparison, in every scene it's seen in. Learned on the work, it covers what the work added too. Two recordings that don't go the same way (one fails where the other didn't) are refused: the scene doesn't start from the same place each time.
5. Before is for the person: this moment as it was, beside how it is now, so the difference is the change, and what the agent changed on the way shows. So each scene is recorded on it with the round it's first recorded in, once the round's scenes have all passed, doing what the round does, as far as the app as it was takes it. What a scene expects of the finished work only waits a moment there, and a scene stops where the app as it was can't do a step, kept up to the step before. Each action the round did is noted by what it was done on, by its role and name as `getByRole` finds it, and on Before, an action that would be done on something else stops the scene there too: a scene is written for the work, and in the app as it was what it looks for can find another thing (the header's last button, Paid where the work put Export; a card where Kan's list menu is), and what it did there isn't how the app was. The same thing is the same role with most of the shorter name's words in the other's, so a card the work gave a badge is still that card. Nobody is asked to judge where Before stopped: the agent's scenes are checked on the round, where they have to pass. Until its round is handed over, a scene's Before follows it: recorded again, in place of what it had, once its file, `playthrough/` or what the round does has changed (`.playthrough/<review>/ran-on-before.json` keeps a hash of the three), and gone from Before once the scene is gone. Once handed over, it's been compared, and Before keeps it. Recording on Before happens in the agent's own working copy, where its env files and packages are: the work is set aside with git stash, Before is checked out with `playthrough/` as it is now, since that's how scenes get into the app and goes with them, not with the work, and the work is put back after, even when recording fails. What changed in the files meanwhile, a file the person saved in their editor or one the app as it was wrote, is kept in a stash of its own rather than let go, and `record` names it and says how to bring it back. The repo's git hooks don't run for any of it, as none of it is anyone's work, and a hook that fails or installs packages on every checkout would stop Before or slow it. Meanwhile every other command that runs the app or keeps it as the work, and taking over from the viewer, is refused, saying to try again once it's done, as the working copy holds the app as it was; one stopped hard is known by its process, on the same start of the machine, so a number another process has after a restart is never taken for it. It's recorded on whatever serves the app, as every round is, so a dev server already running serves the app as it was for that minute, and is given a moment to catch up with the work again before the round is compared, and a Take over meanwhile gets it too. Stopped halfway, Playthrough puts it back before it ends, or the next command does, or it says the two git commands that do. A scene the app as it was can't start has no Before. A scene the browser stopped, not the app (a page that crashed), says nothing about the app: the round isn't recorded, and recording again tries again.
6. Then `record` compares the round with Before and with every round before it, scene by scene, steps matched by title. A change is a thing and what happened to it, in Playthrough's own words, never the agent's: “Export button is new, and moved the Show choice left”, “Status column looks different” where only the pictures differ (a thing that was read moved only if the reading says so: what the picture shows moving on it, as Next's dev indicator over Kan's “Robin Park”, is it looking different, and only what's moved where nothing's read is said to have moved), “New steps: “Export them”” where a scene takes steps it didn't. The same change in several scenes is one change, and in a scene it's seen where the recording first has it on the screen, as the person watching meets it, not where the page first has it: a page read top to bottom showed all twelve of its changes at its first step, below the screen as most were, and now shows them a screen at a time (one the scene never brings into view keeps the first step it's there). Words that kept most of what they said, in order, their start and end two thirds of what they were, are those words reworded wherever they went, and what's marked is what reads differently: the site's close, a line about the video added and moved down the page by a new section above it, is that line, where it was a paragraph gone and another new, three boxes; a paragraph rewritten keeps too little of itself, and is one gone and another new. One thing moved straight up or down on its own, by something above it that took room or gave it, is part of that change, as many moved alike are. A control whose words changed is that control reworded, even a little wider and moved for it (“Clear completed button now says “Clear 2 completed””), not one gone and one new; a list whose items are in a new order is told as the fewest of them that moved, of those in it both times, the rest having only made room: two that traded places have swapped, called by their words, one or two that went past others moved that many places (“STU-9 Print the programme 7th Nov” moved down 2 places, in Kan's sorted list, where it was said to have swapped with one of the two it passed), and more are a new order; other things that traded places, each as many things strong, have swapped too; and words whose box is only narrower or wider, with no background or border to show it, still starting or ending where they did, aren't a change: words that did move on the screen, the pictures find. A person reads a row, a list's item or a card as one thing, so the page is read with its items: what the page says is a row or a list's item, and anything one of several side by side with the same tag and class, as Actual's payees are. Items pair across the rounds by what's the same in both inside them, then, in lists side by side, in order when there are as many; words only in the same place say nothing of which is which. An item says what's read in it, not a menu's options it keeps hidden. A whole item new or gone is one change, called by its words past an avatar's initial (“Maya maya-…@kan-test.local Member” is gone, not its name, its email, its role and its ⋯ each); an item whose words changed in more than one piece is one change saying what it says now (“Apples -- 12 votes” now says “Apples 57% 12 votes”); how something in it looks, or where it is, is told on its own. Inside an item, a thing is known by its place there, its column and which of the things of its kind in it it is, in which kind of item of which list, words reworded by how they changed too, and a move by which way it went, each word with a number in it left out as data; never by its own words, which in an item are its data. So the same change in each item is one change: the polls app's new shares were fourteen changes and are one, in 3 places; Actual's twenty-three changes are four, its new transaction counts one of them: “96”, “2”, “2” and 17 more are new, and moved 20 things left; and “(paid)” after each customer's name is one change, in 6 places, where it was a table with six things changed inside. Six customers each renamed differently are still six changes, counted. A change seen in several scenes is told in the one that joined the review first, not the one whose file sorts first, and a scene recorded on Before again keeps its place there; every round keeps its scenes in that order too, the task's first and one new to the round last, where they went by their files' names and the issue's own scene came last. What changes by itself is kept as the changes it stands for, each by itself, and left out as that, before anything is told together: Kan's members each with a new email every run made the members table one change of eight things, which left the whole table out of every comparison; now it's each email. A part of a picture that changes by itself, known only by where it is, with nothing read there or only near it, is left out only in its own scene: the same place in another scene is another thing, and Kan's invite page lost a real change to a patch that changed by itself on the members page. `record` lists each change since the round before, by its id.

Handed over, that's what it says of each change in the step, kept so, as everything after reads it a change at a time. Asked of each change, it gave seven whys for one phone card's top bar. A scene new this round is asked about too, what's different from Before in it, as nobody has been: #622's phone scene, added in round 3, had gone unexplained. A change seen in several scenes is asked about in the first of them, in the order they joined, as the person meets it. Each change that Playthrough can name, that is: one only the pictures show, with nothing read there changed (“Robin Park looks different”, under Next's dev indicator), is the person's to look at, as they see what it is at a glance and the agent can't see more of it than Playthrough did; it's outlined on the recording as every change is, and `record` tells the agent of it in a line that asks nothing, unless it's what the agent changed for a note, which it can still say, so it shows in the answer (the sample's “Message field looks different”). Read in any scene, a change is one Playthrough can name. It answers each note there too, where `record` left a reply for each with empty words, placed where the note's thing is on the new recording: found by pairing what was read at the same step, never said by the agent. A note made in the app is answered on a fork of the scene it was taken over in, written by the agent: what the person did there, as steps of the agent's own, finding things by role and name as a scene does, `test.fork("<what they do>", { notes: ["<id>"] }, body)` in the scene's file, as only that file is loaded when the scene runs. `record` runs the scene to where they took it over, then the fork, recorded as a scene is (each of its steps read at its end, the page glimpsed after each action), and places the reply where the fork first shows what the note is on, by the same role and most of the same words, the closest where several have them (a reminder's Subject passes for its Message otherwise); where it never does, at the fork's end. A round with a note made in the app that has no fork, or whose fork fails, isn't recorded: `record` says how to write it, from the step it starts in, as a scene that fails stops a round. The fork is the note's, not a scene of the review: it isn't listed or compared as one, it's run while one of its notes is open, and once they're settled it's gone. In the viewer it's the fork you made, run again: drawn off the scene where you took it over, its steps the agent's, your notes on it where it shows their things, compared with the fork you made at each note, “what you did” beside “done again”. A fork is a detour off its scene: playing goes up it where a note on it waits, and from its end comes back down to where it left the scene and plays the rest, so the scene's own end is never skipped for the next scene; the arrow keys step from scene to scene, never onto a fork, and past a fork's end they're back on its scene; a fork that's gone, its last note removed or on the round you go to, leaves you where it left its scene, not on the first scene (with the fork gone, the timeline had drawn none of the scene's forks until a reload). An answer's card on a fork closes once it's played past, as any mark's does. A fork run again isn't taken over: a fork is taken over from its scene, never from another one, so Take over is greyed there; Take over where you were, on a round that doesn't answer the note, takes the scene over where you did and runs its fork, not your clicks. This replaced walking the person's own steps again by where they clicked, which broke as the app changed: found by place alone, with two buttons swapped the walk clicked Mark as paid; renamed, it stopped short, and the answer hung where the steps stopped, with a line the person could do nothing with. What the person did is done again only on the round they did it, the same app, as Take over where you were does there. A thing noted in the app is called as a scene's reading calls it, by its label (the textbox “Message”, not the words in it), so the fork finds it by the same name. A box an editor draws to write in, as Kan's comment box, is what's noted when it's clicked, not the part of the page around it, and with no name of its own, as an empty one has, it's noted by the spot. A scene that's new to the reviewer can have a `line` at its start: what it starts from, or the choice it shows. A bug the agent can't make happen is still a round: the agent records the scene that does what the report says, says in its line that it couldn't make it happen and how it tried, and hands it over, so the person can take the scene over and show it, their steps coming back as a note made in the app, its fork run every round; on Kan's #643, a Firefox-only cursor jump, the agent asked four good questions and kept the round to itself, so nobody could show it the bug. Recording again keeps what it wrote.

9. `playthrough wait` hands the round over, which the rules allow once every note has an answer and every step changed since the round before (in a scene new this round, since Before) has its account; if one hasn't, it says which, with all that changed there in Playthrough's words. Handed over, it says to tell the person it's theirs to review, then, as the notes come, where their answers go. Where nothing changed in what the scenes show, `record` says so in those words, and that what they don't show, as a colour on hover or a tooltip never opened, isn't compared, rather than “Nothing changed”. `record` says how to word an account, and to hand the round over in the background, in a line each, only the first time the review is recorded; after that, what's still to do says what's missing, rather than the same paragraph every round. Then it waits for a send that asks for something and prints each note to answer, with its conversation so far, whose each verdict is, and a picture of the page as the person saw it, what the note is on outlined, or for a note on the whole scene, the page the scene ended on; a reply missing its words says it's the reply's “words”, under “replies”. A note made in the app, taken over, has no picture: `wait` says what the person did there instead, and the step they took the scene over in, with how to write it as a fork. It names the review and where its scenes are, for an agent new to it. While it waits, it keeps a file named by its process in the review's `waiting/`, so the viewer knows: sent with no agent waiting, as when the agent's own time limit ended its wait in the background, the foot says “Sent, no agent is waiting · tell your agent”, which knows to run `playthrough wait` from `record`, and back to “Sent, waiting for the agent” within two seconds of one starting.
10. In the viewer, each person watches, leaves notes, says Looks good or replies to each answer and Looks good to each scene, and sends. A reply is words back, whatever they are: what's still not right, or an answer to a question the agent asked ("Want it in this PR?"); it judges nothing, so a plain answer never goes out as a complaint. Looks good is the only verdict. A note has one state for everyone, the last word on its answer, whoever said it and under their name: Looks good settles it, and a reply, even after someone's Looks good, sends it back to the agent. An answer waits for whoever asked for it, by writing the note or sending it back; anyone can judge it. Looks good on a scene, and accepting, are each person's own. Anyone can accept once every scene is good for them and nothing waits for the agent, and `wait` says the review is done.

## The page

A review opens on one page (`packages/viewer/src/Work.tsx`, each scene in `Scene.tsx`): its title and what was asked, a line saying how many scenes and who recorded them, and where your notes stand, a dot in its colour and a count each (2 to send, 1 to check, 1 with the agent, 5 settled), each going to the first such note, as a pull request says how many conversations are still open; then each scene the work meets, its recording on the left and on the right its way through, a list of stops (`Steps.tsx`), and last the review's end, where what you've said is sent. The page scrolls as any page does, with your hand, slow or fast, and a scene is held at the window's top while you go through it: its name, recording and way stay put, every stop a line, the same distance apart, the one you're on open (a step with its line, the agent's why and what was said in it; an answer with its thread and Looks good or Reply; the end with Note on the whole scene), opening out of its line as the one you left folds back, on a surface of its own while the rest are quieter, and what moves is where you are on the way. Laid out open, every step's why, every thread and every answer's buttons stood beside each other, some to read and some to press ("too many different things going on in the sidebar, some of which are actionable, some that aren't"); and one surface gliding from stop to stop trailed behind them as they opened and folded. Each stop is the same stretch of scrolling, 100px, a turn of the wheel, whatever's in it: the stop you're on is the one whose stretch the page is in, on whichever round you're looking at; come to rest, the page is put in the middle of that stretch, which nothing on it shows, so the next turn goes one stop either way however far into a stretch the last one ended, two quick turns go two, and a fling runs through several. Past a scene's last stop the page goes on, the scene leaving whole, name, recording and way, and the next coming up whole. Before, a way slid past a line near the window's top beside a pinned recording, and every stop's room was the scrolling it took: a step was a turn and a note's thread three, two of them on a note whose start had gone off the top; the surface marking where you were slid back against your scroll to catch up, and the page nudged itself onto a stop you'd rested just off; by Looks good the whole way had gone off the top, and a recording left with an empty column beside it (“something just feels inconsistent and unsatisfying and off when scrolling through the page and seeing the sidebar”). A scene's way ends where its recording does, its bar with it, and fades out just past it, always, so a stop opening out never shows past it before it's measured, and the last one, gone up as far as it goes, is clear of the fade (run on to the window's bottom, notes went on below the recording). A way longer than that goes up to bring the stop you're on to a fifth of the way down, as the page brings a scene up, until its end is clear of the foot, fading out under the window's top: the only time it moves. Going up only as far as the stop needed left a stop late in a long way at the window's bottom ("so far down"). A scene coming up or going away is nobody's: nothing plays while it moves, nothing closes in on a note or a change, and its bar waits (“the recording always needs to be in its settled spot before/while steps are playing”). Scrolled back above a scene, it's before its first stop again, as it was arrived at, however fast the page went past it; scrolled back up into one, it's held again at its end. What scrolling passes plays nothing (a step shows its start; the first, where its page has drawn itself, as a recording begins on the blank page the app is still loading into, until what the app adds pauses for a beat, and its line fills from there, not from the blank page); still 0.18s, the stop you're on plays. Space or ↓ goes one stop on, ↑ one back, and a stop pressed is gone to: in the scene that's held at once, as nothing on the page moves but where you are, and into another as the page glides there a frame at a time (the browser's smooth scroll could be cut short by another), playing once it's held. Your hand on the wheel lets the glide go. On a phone the page scrolls as any page and a stop is gone to by pressing it.

- **What's different from Before**, under the title, is the review's index, as a pull request lists the files it changed (`Parts.tsx`; on the branch `what-is-different`, tried on #500's copy). Scenes stay the page; this only points into them. A part is a page, known by its largest heading, or the sidebar or the top bar around every page; each is one still however many scenes pass it, framed on what changed from the picture its step ended on, with the diamond's name, Playthrough's words for it, and how many scenes it's in ("In 2 scenes"). A page Before never reached is new, and everything seen on it is that page, as it has nothing to be compared with (on #500, twelve changes on the phone scene's first step are My cards, a new page); a scene Before couldn't begin is new from its first step. Pointing at a still shows Before there, labelled as Compare labels it. Pressing it goes to the step it's seen best at (on a computer's screen where it can be; a new page where the most is on it), and there to the change, close on it, beside Before; a new page plays into its step, as Before had nothing there. Once you've come to rest at a step where it's seen, in any scene, its still is quieter, as a file marked viewed: worked out from where you've been this visit, never kept. Where the review's end offers to send or accept, it says once what you haven't seen, each part's name going to it: "1 change you haven't seen · My cards". A change is never judged on its own: Looks good is still a scene's. (A first try at the change as the page, each part a scene's section with its scenes as variants, is on the branch `each-change`, left: it read the scene through the change, its Looks good said of a scene it didn't name, a new page, most of a feature, was a scene cut short, and a second round fell back to scenes.)
- **Always the latest, as a pull request shows its latest push.** Rounds are the agent's turns, not places to go: the page is the latest round, and one that comes in is followed. What was is Compare's, on the bar, for what you're on (Before, or at an answer, When you said it), never a round's number. Each note is one thread, as it stands, wherever it's met. Once it asks nothing of you, settled, or with the agent or someone else, it's a line in its step, its first words and where it stands, as a resolved conversation is, there when you're on the step, and opens when pressed, Settled folding it again; as stops, a way three rounds on was as many settled notes as steps, each gone through. One whose scene is gone is listed at the page's end, Outdated; Outdated · List name field is gone over a note whose thing had gone read as "no longer matters" over the answer that asked Does this fix it?, and went. At the end, How it got here is a line each time the agent recorded it (Recorded again by Claude Code at 13:47, answering 4 notes · 1 note sent back), as a pull request tells its pushes. Before, you could go back to any round, and the whole page became it: its recordings, each note as it stood then, without the answers after, and notes of your own written on it; a note settled on an earlier round left the latest page and was found only there, so one note had a version for every round ("I feel a bit lost figuring out the state of things").
- **Scenes that look good to you**, nothing changed since you said so, arrive folded to the picture they end on, small, with Looks good's square and their name under it, side by side with the others that look good (a line of names alone looked out of place beside the open scenes); a press opens one. Looks good at the end of the last open scene stays there: there's no next stop to go on to, and going past it scrolled the recording half away.
- **The stops**, in the order they come: each step, and after it each note in it that's open for you, its answer waiting on you or what you said on it not sent yet, at its moment; then those about the whole scene; and last, the scene's end, always there, with notes or without. The end holds Looks good while nothing of yours in the scene is unsent or with the agent; otherwise it says where that stands in its place ("1 note to send", "With the agent", "1 waiting for your answer"), so the end never just goes missing. Under it, while you're there, Note on the whole scene, written there in place, at the end, as a stop of its own. A rail runs down through their signs (a step's number, a note's dot in its colour, Looks good's square), filled as far as you've come: the rail's numbers are the steps' alone, as a note's number beside them read as one more step, and a note's pin on the recording is its dot too; the rail and each step's line are drawn in a tone of their own, the track, so they read in the dark too. The stop you're on sits on the page's surface beside its sign, its own surface, opening and folding with it; the others' words are quieter, their signs whole on the rail, and before you've gone along a scene all are, folded, as you're on none. At the end with Looks good, the surface takes its green and the words are its button, with ↵; its square on the rail grows a little rather than wearing a ring. The stop you're on opens out of its line as the one before folds back: a step, with a line of its own that fills as it plays and is dragged along for the moments between, only there (on every step, it was a line each to look past); a note, its thread, folded to its first words and To check or To send, the words the line under the title counts it by, with Does this fix it? · Looks good · Reply where an answer waits on you, and once someone's Looks good is sent, Settled, with a quiet Reply to send it back, rather than the question asked again under the Looks good that answered it; what changed new to you in a step is no stop of its own but met under the step's line, where its diamond is: the agent's why for the step, its name said once over all it says there (every why it gave, where it said why a change at a time, as it once was asked to, or an earlier round's changes are new to you too), or, made for a note in another scene, what the agent answered it did for it, the note's dot after it (Changed for your note alone, before, told the reviewer nothing), or Changed here where it said nothing; pressed, or its diamond on the line, the recording rests at its moment close on it. Compare is the bar's alone, for what you're on: how it was before the work, as a pull request's diff is against where it branched; at an answer, the note's own then, the recording as it was when it was last said, written, sent back or judged, named When you said it, as before the work often had nothing of what it's on. Beside each answer and each change in the way, it was a second and a third Compare, each comparing with something else. What's new since you last said something about the scene is marked and kept apart, never compared with: Compare beside a change once showed the round it was new since, named Round 2, then Last time, a third side counted from a different round in each scene and for each person, none of them seen ("are we introducing more combinatorial states again?"), and what changed is outlined a part of the page at a time, the things close together as one (`regions.ts`: the phone card's top bar, not its five pieces). It said a count ("7 changes"), which pointed at nothing you could read, and showed the agent's first why while outlining all seven. The recording is where what changed shows, and Compare there how it was, so the way says why rather than showing it again: a picture of each part, as it was over as it is, was tried under its step and taken out. What changed only off the screen a step was read on, which its recording can't show (the phone's board, its Done column past the screen's edge), isn't met there. Steps new to you are one stretch, said once above its first rather than told as a change (the phone scene's Tap Marked done had read Changed here): 3 new steps, or New step, beside a hairline, so the count says where the stretch ends; the agent's why for them is under the first one's line, where every step's why is. It was · New after each title; then the why was under the divider too, between the steps on its own, where it read as neither a step's nor the scene's ("it feels odd"). As a stop of its own each change was drawn twice, a diamond on the line and a row of its own, and the phone scene was seven sparse stops; folded, it's four. A change made for a note is met in each scene it shows in: in the note's own, with its answer; in any other, as changed for it, everything made for the note there outlined together (#525's tabs, and Submit comment they pushed down), the pin on the first its comparison found. The list spreads out beside the recording, up to a comfortable gap, and is never taller than it: what's longer scrolls inside it. New steps sit under the first of them, where they start playing, not under the step before, whose result is where they begin.
- **The recording answers each stop.** A step plays into its result and rests there. At a note or a change, the recording is still at its moment and glides in close on what it's on, its pins drawn once it's there (on its way, none are: not left behind, nor in the corner that's for a thing not found), framed in what it sits in (the panel or card around it that draws itself: inside it where it's bigger than the view, the whole of it where it's smaller), and back out to the whole page as the next step plays. A phone's recording is the phone's shape: as tall as the window has room for, as narrow as the phone, against its way, its name over it as wide as it (in the landscape picture every scene had, it was laid across the width, a phone's top three and a half times as close); Compare brings a second phone in beside it, each the whole screen, and a mark on something past the screen's edge, as a board's columns, isn't drawn. A small picture comes closer, up to three times, so what it's on can be read. Compare (C), on the bar, puts how it was beside it (Before, or at an answer, When you said it): at a note or a change, Now close on it in its half and Before on the same part of the page, at #643's change Now's "Write an|outline of the post" beside Before's "Woutline of the postrite an|", large enough to read; elsewhere each half shows the page's whole height, across it where the scene works, the same for all of a scene's steps: what each step changed or acted on and where the hand went, with the row or panel each is in, so a step's view never cuts through the column it happens in and nothing moves between steps (#525's "Read the moves and the talk together" was so close it cut the names off its feed). Now's view glides in step with its picture narrowing, and back as it widens, on the same curve from the same frame: what glides is the part of the page shown, how close that is following from the picture's width, so it never zooms past where it ends and comes back. Pins on the same thing, a note and a change, sit side by side. A pin whose thing isn't on the frame isn't drawn: in the recording's corner it covered the app, and what's said is in the way beside it. Compared, Before (or the round compared with) follows the step click for click where both clicked as often in it, so each click lands as its other does; else evenly through the step. A recording keeps the page, not its pixels, so the viewer's browser lays it out again: it's played with no scrollbars, as it was recorded with none, and its window is kept as wide as it was recorded, widened by a fraction where a zoom made it narrower (`#fit`): at some zooms it came out under Kan's breakpoint at exactly the recorded 1280px, and the app moved its search field to a row of its own. Typing into a field, the caret is drawn after as many of the field's letters as it was recorded after (its `letters`, kept since 5 Nov; before that, at their end, where typing mostly is), as the viewer's browser lays them out (`#onField`), told which field by what rrweb kept typed into: Firefox laid Kan's overflowing header out 42px right of the Chromium it was recorded in, and a caret where the recording measured it sat in the words. The pointer, which recorded goes to the middle of what it clicks, is moved by as far as what it clicks next, or clicked last, is from where it was then (`#aimedAt`), measured on whichever of what got the click and what holds it, up to the control, it was nearest the middle of. The outlines are still where the recording measured them. Drawing text at its exact size (`text-rendering: geometricPrecision`) was tried for a caret that touched its last letter, and taken out: it moved Chromium's own layout 7px from what it recorded.
- **Looks good moves you on; words keep you where you said them.** Looks good on an answer or at a scene's end is seen where it was said, and after 0.6 s the page goes on to the next stop, unless you've gone on already. A reply or a note stays where you wrote it, so you see it saved in place (a Not quite once took the reviewer to another scene before it showed). At an answer, Enter says Looks good and R opens a reply, heard before anything else on the page; Send is Ctrl+Enter (⌘↵), so no plain Enter ever sends. Where a scene stands is said once, at its end, its last stop: said again over its way too, it was said over what said it. Pressed, Looks good gives the keyboard back to the page, so the next Enter is heard where you are, never by the button left behind.
- **Notes are written in the list.** N, then a click on what it's about (the pointer says Note on this), puts the note's pin there and opens its words as a stop of their own, the next after where you are, with the keyboard in it; nothing is drawn over the recording. Let go unsaved, you're back on the stop you were on. Finish your note at the review's end brings its scene up with the note open.
- **Taking it over is a click.** At rest, pointing at the recording says Take over as Maja beside your pointer, and a click takes it over there, as T does; the app is made ready only then. While the app is yours, the list rests at a third, but for what you say there: a note made in the app is written in the list too. Back from the app, it's kept ready, and the recording takes a click again.
- **An answer to a note made in the app** is checked the way the note was found. At its stop, what you did there plays over the recording, Showing what you did, done again said above it, never on it: the fork the agent recorded on this round's work (`playthrough forks`), from where you took the scene over to the end of the step its reply says to watch, resting on the page there; where none is named, to the answer's moment, close on its thing. Arrived on, it rests where it ends, still, and plays when you go to it. Compare there puts beside it what you did then, your own recording of that round from where you took it over to where you noted it, and plays both again from the start, click for click where both made as many, each on the things clicked and typed in: at #525's note 2, Before's half-written comment is gone when you come back from Activity and Now's is still there. Taken over there, the app is opened where you took it over and what you did is done again, the note's pin on its thing.
- **The way goes through what's new to you.** Two or more steps in a row with nothing new to you (nothing changed in them since you last said something about the scene, or since Before where you never have, as the round's comparison found it in this scene wherever the change is told, what isn't real and what was made for a note on it aside; and no note there still open, whether waiting on you, the agent or someone else, or unsent) are one stop, its sign the steps' numbers: on a scene you said something about before, Nothing new since last time, the notes settled in them each a line under its step's title; else, where Before has it, Unchanged, the work having changed nothing in them. It plays them through, and while you're on it lists their titles, each a way in: pressed, they're each a stop again, and that one is gone to. Go through each step did that from the first, a link to press among lines to read. Changes made for a note are seen with its answer, so they don't count. A scene the work left as it was is one such stop and Looks good.
- **The review's end** is the way's last stop, under the last scene's: ↓ past a scene's end goes on to it, and so does saying Looks good or saving a note at the last stop of all. It's where what you've said goes, all at once, as a pull request's review is submitted at its foot: Send with what's ready (Ctrl+Enter, from anywhere on the page), the notes it sends listed under it, each going to where it was written; even once it's all good, so accepting is never the only way to send what you said; then All good · Accept the work, by a click only, as the keys you sent with must never accept the review (a reviewer's Ctrl+Enter, Send for two rounds, became Accept on the third); and once you've sent, what the agent is doing, where Send was. It's under how it got here, on the left, as a pull request's review box is under its story; after a scene still being gone through, it's held at the window's top as a scene is, so that scene leaves whole, and with every scene looking good, folded to its picture, it's just under them. Closing the page with the work's title and what was asked again, beside Send, read as the page starting over ("So weird"). Send used to float at the window's bottom right, away from where you'd just written: "it's a bit unclear what the user should do after adding a note on a step in a scene, it's not obvious that they should look down to the foot and click". Now only Go through it, a step at a time floats there, before you've begun and while a scene doesn't look good to you yet (not on a phone, which scrolls as any page), on a plate of the page's ground fading in above it, so the way's words pass under it quietly. A stop pressed brings its scene up to the window's top.

## Taking a scene over

```bash
playthrough take-over <review> "<scene>" ["<step>"]
```

In the viewer it's Take over (T), on the action bar across the frame's bottom edge: while the latest round plays, its scene runs alongside in the background, as far as the playhead has gone and no further, so paused, the moment the playhead rests on is ready a beat later and loaded unseen while you look (the keyboard stays with you: an app that puts its text cursor somewhere as it loads gives it back to what had it, a note you've started writing), and Take over turns the frame itself into the app there, at the same size and place, the time saying Live. It's where you paused, to the action, or the scene's start while its name is up: a recording keeps when each of a step's actions was done, the scene runs as far as the recording had got, and what the scene's actions changed only in the page's memory, like a search typed or a form opened, in this step or the ones before it, is done again in your browser before it shows, while one that saved something or went to another address isn't done twice, nor anything before it, as loading the page brings those back, and it's scrolled where the recording was at that moment, the page and each part of it that scrolls on its own, as Kan's card does inside its page, found by the path back to it, and light or dark, still or moving, as recorded, a scroll not being an action it stops at (a page being read, taken over partway down, opened a screen up); a scene recorded on a phone is a finger's, so there a mouse drag is a swipe: what's under it follows the pointer, and let go, glides on as a fling does, to the column it snaps to where it snaps, as Kan's board does on a phone, where a wheel or a trackpad only jumped it a column at a time ("It just jumps from one column to the next"); pressed and held first, it's the app's own drag, as a long press is on a phone, and a click is still a click; the recording's pointer, whose tag names whoever has the scene at that moment, and, handed on as it plays, changes its name as it does here, growing to it, so the hand-off is seen where both pages look alike, comes to yours and gives way to it, the name going with it (“You, as a customer”); Taking over is doing: from Take over until you're back, the app is over all the recording draws, the veil of a scene's start or end too, and what drives the recording rests: the recording itself, where a click or a pin does nothing; All scenes over it and the row under it (the timeline, which still says Live and grows your fork, speed and size), stepping back to a third in the app; an open card about it, gone in the app, where it would cover what you're doing, and back as it was when you are (one about the app, a note made there or being tried, still answers); and the keys with them, so only the app, Note and Back answer, and a note chosen from the bar goes back to the recording at it. Escape, in the app or out of it, lays the recording back over it, so what was open there stays open, and T again carries on where you were; but it's the app's first, as N is while you type: if the app does something with it, closing a menu, a list or a dialog, that's all it does; in a field you're typing in, it leaves the field; and the next Escape, which it lets be, goes back. A run can't go back, so going back before it starts another. One app is made ready for each review at a time: making the next one ready, at the next pause, gives back the one before, taken over or not, and its address goes with it, so a session of pauses leaves two worlds in the app at most, the one ready and the run following the playhead. A scene whose file says it reaches outside the app (a Playwright annotation on the scene or a `describe()` around it, `{ type: "reaches outside", description: "sends Sam an email" }`) is never run ahead: not alongside the playhead, nor as it rests, only when it's taken over, as every pause would otherwise send that email again. The runs are Playthrough's own: each is a window of its own, the one its scene is recorded in (a phone's screen and touch, say, or another language, when the scene's file says so with `test.use`, in the file or a `describe()`), with its own cookies and storage, in one headless browser Playthrough keeps open while it runs, so a new one opens in a moment and nothing is kept waiting; following Kan costs one Chrome, about 350 MB, where a Playwright run of its own for each, with a spare, held about a gigabyte. Recording still runs the scenes as Playwright tests. Asked for before it's ready, the recording's pointer says Taking over… and, once a run taken over has got there before, counts down to it, “Taking over in 3…”, a second at a time: each run taken over keeps how long it took from each moment to the next, not counting waiting for the playhead, which on the same app is the same each time to within a few percent, and how long it took to hand the page over once it got there, and the viewer adds the quickest the app has loaded in the frame, as one slow load, a page the app builds the first time, isn't every load; a run following the playhead isn't counted, as it keeps everything to do again and its page settles while it waits. If the app isn't there when the count is done, the pointer says Taking over… until it is. A run told where it will be taken over goes straight through the steps before it, not waiting for the playhead, so a moment deep in Kan's longest scene is ready in about four seconds, not nine. From the command line, it runs the scene to the end of that step (or of the whole scene) on the app as it is now, quickly and unrecorded, from what every scene starts from, then hands its page to you at http://localhost:5830/take-over/ and a name made up for that moment, which no one could guess: the same address, cookies and what the app kept in the browser, so you carry on by hand in the real app, from a fresh copy of that moment. The app is passed on through Playthrough while the command runs, on every network this machine is on, so a phone on the same one can open it too, but only that phone: opening the moment's address from elsewhere hands a pass with the page, which the app never sees, and anyone else there is turned away. Taken over in the viewer, the app is passed on to this machine alone. Either way it answers only to this machine's names and addresses, so a site that has made its name lead here gets nothing, and only a page served from this machine, as the viewer is, may read what the app sends through it, as a recording made there asks the app for its fonts and pictures again. On Spliit, with the app already running, it's ready in about three seconds; on Kan too, where the setup signs a new user up through Kan's own sign-in, so the session is Kan's real one, a cookie the page can't read, and it carries over with nothing special for Kan. The page runs on the recordings' clock, so times on it read as they did there. Playthrough loads the review's scenes itself to take them over, with Node, and finds a module of their own that one imports as Playwright does when it records them: without its ending (“../history”), or by its .js name, and loads it again with them whenever it or they change, so a helper the agent rewrites in a later round isn't run as it was. Only the app as it is now can be taken over, not Before.

## Downloading a scene as a video

In the viewer it's Download video (V), at the right of the line the review's title is on, there in a scene and gone on every scene at once: the scene you're on, as it's shown, alone or side by side with the round Compare shows, as an MP4 your browser downloads, named for the scene and its rounds (“Send a reminder · Round 7 and Round 8”). It's for a pull request, a teammate, a post: something that plays anywhere, with nothing to install. `playthrough open` films it in a browser of its own (`packages/cli/src/video.ts`): the viewer plays the scene there with nothing but its recordings (`packages/viewer/src/Video.tsx`), opening on its name over the softened page as in the viewer, and closing on its last frame softened the same way, with the logo and playthrough.sh where the name stood. Chromium films a page only at its screen's size, so the page draws itself at the size the video is, no wider than 1920 and no taller than 1920, never more than twice its own size; drawn no larger, Chromium hands over a frame every 60th of a second the page changes, so it's filmed as it plays, and each frame goes into the video as it's filmed, at 60 frames a second, as smooth as a screen shows it, in HD video's own colours (BT.709, in the range a TV shows, which every player takes a video to be: the browser's own conversion kept a page's full range, and players that took it as a TV's blew the contrast out), with that browser's own H.264 (`packages/cli/src/mp4.ts` writes the file). So it takes about as long as the video, a second more: 16 seconds for “Send a reminder”, ten seconds of scene with its name and its end.

Making it, the button says so where you pressed it, as taking over does (`packages/viewer/src/DownloadVideo.tsx`): “Downloading in 14…”, counting down a second at a time with only the number changing, filling from the left as it goes, and ending on a tick, “Downloaded”, as your browser takes the file, then back. `open` sends what it has left as it's made, a line at a time, then the video: the playing still to go, and the frames waiting to go in.

## Working on Playthrough

`npm test` holds the review model, the comparison, the command line and what the viewer shows each person to their promises, in about a minute and a half. The comparison's rules are each tested on two small pages, and the command line is run for real in Chromium, as two stories side by side, each in a copy of the example made a git repo. While iterating, `npm run quick` runs everything but those two stories, in about ten seconds; run `npm test` before each commit. `npm run check` checks the types. While working on the viewer, `npm run dev --workspace @playthrough/viewer` serves it with instant reloads beside a running `playthrough open`.

Working on it needs Node 24 or later, which runs the TypeScript as it is. The package runs on Node 22.18 or later, which runs an app's setup and scenes as the TypeScript they're written in, and says so on anything older.
