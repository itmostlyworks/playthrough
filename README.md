# Vantage

The agent says it's done. You decide if it's good.

Vantage records every flow your agent's change touched, before and after, including what the agent didn't mention. Click what's wrong, and the next try lands right beside it. It runs on your machine, with any coding agent that has a shell.

Early work. What's here so far:

- **`packages/review`**: what a review is, and the store that keeps it. A review is rounds the agent records and sends people make, each with one writer. The store takes the loop's steps (start, save a round, take the notes, finish, send, accept, wait) and refuses anything that breaks a rule, saying what's wrong in plain words. It keeps reviews in memory or in a folder. Several people can review, and each person's judgement is their own: what's new to you, which notes wait for you, and whether a flow is good for you are worked out for each person, never kept.
- **`packages/page`**: what's on a page, as a person would name it, and the same thing found again. A reading is the page a step left, everything on it named in Vantage's fixed words; two readings of the same step on two rounds pair thing by thing, which is how the comparison finds what changed, where an answer lands, and where a note sits in every round.
- **`packages/cli`**: the commands an agent runs, over the store, with reviews kept in `.vantage/` in the app's repo. It records each flow, reads the page each step leaves, compares each round with every round before it, walks the person's steps in the app again for each note made there, places each answer where its note's thing is on the new round, and pictures each note made on a recording for the agent.
- **`packages/viewer`**: the page a person reviews in: the recording, an action bar across its bottom edge (Note, Compare, Take over, and this flow's notes), and under it the timeline, the speed and the size, with the foot beside them. A round's flows play like chapters, each opening with its name over the page its first step left, softened, the page its picture shows, and the agent's line about it under the agent's name, where playing waits the first time you come to it; a click plays and pauses, as a video's does; the recording fits, or shows at actual size as a window that follows the pointer or stays where you drag it; the timeline answers the pointer and the drag; and the line over the frame says which round you're looking at. What's new to you since you last looked at a flow is a diamond on the timeline where it starts, new steps too; stopped on one, what changed is outlined on the recording, and new steps on what they click or type into. What the agent changed for a note in its own flow is in the note's answer instead: its dot is the one mark for both. The notes button lists this flow's notes, rising from the bar, a line each, lit on the timeline as you point at it. Opened, from the timeline or the list, a note goes still on the frame it was written on and a change on the frame it's seen on, or it plays its stretch (new steps, a note on a stretch, or a note whose answer moves, in the step its reply names from a second before the first action that set what it's on moving, nothing stopping it on the way). What's open sits beside its spot, covering neither what it's on nor that thing in the round beside it, and can be dragged anywhere and closed with its ×. A note's card is the conversation: what was said, what the agent changed for it under its answer (each played and outlined when picked), and “Does this fix it?” with Compare beside it (Looks good plays on; a saved Not quite stays open, paused, your words in it, until Enter plays on); a change's card is its why in the agent's words, and with several changes at one moment, pointing at one's name outlines just it. Compare (C), held, shows this moment as it was, in place, on the round you last looked at the flow on or else Before (for an answer, the round its note was written or sent back on); pressed, the two play together side by side, or one above the other, whichever shows each larger, until it's pressed again or Escape. At a flow's end it asks over its last frame whether it's good: Looks good, which settles the answers in it you've seen (one you haven't is shown first), or Not quite, a note on the whole flow. The foot names one step from where the playhead is (Play on, Next, Send, Accept), Enter takes it and Ctrl+Enter accepts, and once you've sent it follows the agent; under it a hairline for each flow turns green as it looks good to you, its tick travelling down from the flow's question. A note you start and leave with words in it is kept, not saved, and the foot offers to finish it. With more than one flow, you arrive on every flow at once: under the work's name and the round, a row for each flow (a small picture, its name and length, and where it stands, in words), the frame beside them showing the one you point at, and under it the one step, Start checking; choosing one grows its picture into the frame, and All flows over the recording, Escape or accepting brings you back. With Note on (N), what a click would note is outlined, named as Vantage names everything (“Settle up button”, “$600.00”, the “Who owes whom” area), and Alt with the wheel widens it to what holds it; a click on empty space is named by what's near it. A note sits on its thing in every round, its pin just outside the outline; playing is watching, so while anything plays, everything drawn over the recording fades (cards, pins, outlines, the line between them, the timeline's words, and the bar, which comes back while the pointer moves over the recording, as a video's controls do), and comes back where it was once it stops, the card kept by its pin, where it stands until the recordings' layout changes (Compare on or off, the window resized) and it glides to a place clear of what it's on and, where it can, of what changed, the side it opened on first; beside it, the earlier round outlines the same thing, for a change too; an open note's answer is outlined in its colour wherever it changed, every place of alike things changed alike, on both rounds; at actual size the view brings what you chose into view; and a note's card greys when what it's on leaves the page. Your note on a change grows out of the change's card: your words under its name, its colour a dot on the diamond, and opened, Compare under your words. On a phone the page runs down the screen: the recording at the top as large as the width lets it be, its controls under it and the foot under them, and every flow at once is its list, then the frame, scrolled as one.
- **`example`**: a small invoices app to try it on.

## The loop

```bash
vantage start <review> "<title>"   # what was asked, in the person's words
vantage start <review>             # once its flows are written: Before, the app as it is
vantage record <review>            # records every flow, the way a person would use it
vantage wait <review>              # hands the round over, then waits for the notes to answer
vantage walk <review>              # walks the person's steps in the app again, on the app as it is now
vantage open [<review>]            # the viewer, on this machine
```

1. The agent starts a review and writes its flows in `.vantage/<review>/flows/` before it changes anything. They show what was asked, so they're written from the task, not the code: by a fresh subagent given the task and what the screens look like, where the agent can start one. Each flow is one Playwright test for each thing a person does, with a `test.step` for each part of it, importing `test` and `expect` from `@mostlyworks/vantage/test`. The test's name is the flow's. `vantage` alone prints everything an agent needs to know, once (`packages/cli/INSTRUCTIONS.md`); each command then says what's next.
2. `vantage start <review>` again records Before: each flow on the app as it is, as far as the app can take it. What a flow expects of the finished work only waits a moment there, and a flow stops only where the app can't do a step, kept up to that step. A flow the browser stopped, not the app (a page that crashed), fails instead, and running it again tries again. Before is recorded once, and what it was recorded from is kept as a commit, with whatever wasn't committed yet.
3. The agent changes the app. `vantage record` starts it as `vantage/app.json` says (`{ "start": "<command>", "url": "<address>" }`), runs each flow with rrweb in every page, the pointer gliding to what it acts on and a beat after each action, and keeps each recording with where its steps start. Each step ends once the page is at rest, and the page it left is read there as the app shows it, not as the pointer hovers it: everything a person would name, with its box and its look, and a picture. A flow that fails records nothing, and `record` names it and the step it failed at. The fonts, images and stylesheets each page loads are kept with its recording, once each by what's in them, and the viewer plays them from there, so a recording looks the same with the app gone or on another machine; of the sizes an image offers, it shows the one it was recorded at.
4. Every flow starts from the same place each round. Vantage starts it at noon on the day the review started, the clock running on from there, in English and UTC, with the page's random numbers from a fixed seed. The rest is the app's, in `vantage/setup.ts`, written once and committed beside `app.json`: its default export runs before each flow, given the page and Playwright's request, and what it returns is the flow's `setup` (the group it opens, say). A flow is recorded twice the first time it's recorded, on the same app, and what differs between the two changes by itself, like a time the server stamps: `start` or `record` says what it is, and it's left out of every comparison of that flow. Two recordings that don't go the same way (one fails where the other didn't) are refused: the flow doesn't start from the same place each time.
5. A flow added since Before is recorded on Before too, as far as the app as it was can take it. That happens in the agent's own working copy, where its env files and packages are: the work is set aside with git stash, Before is checked out, and the work is put back after, even when recording fails. Stopped halfway, Vantage puts it back before it ends, or the next command does, or it says the two git commands that do. A flow the app as it was can't start has no Before, and isn't tried again.
6. Then `record` compares the round with Before and with every round before it, flow by flow, steps matched by title. A change is a thing and what happened to it, in Vantage's own words, never the agent's: “Export button is new, and moved the Show choice left”, “Status column looks different” where only the pictures differ, “New steps: “Export them”” where a flow takes steps it didn't. The same change in several flows is one change. `record` lists each change since the round before, by its id.
7. The agent says why each of them is there in the round's file, `.vantage/<review>/rounds/<n>/round.json`, under `accounts` by the change's id: its line, the note it was made for, or why nothing really changed. It answers each note there too, where `record` left a reply for each with empty words, placed where the note's thing is on the new recording: found by pairing what was read at the same step, never said by the agent. A note made in the app is answered where the person's steps lead: `record` runs the flow to where they took it over, does their steps again after it, recorded, and places the reply where they got to; where their steps stop short, it says where, and the agent fixes what the step is on or says why in its answer. A flow that's new to the reviewer can have a `line` at its start: what it starts from, or the choice it shows. Recording again keeps what it wrote.
8. Each round says which agent recorded it. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Gemini CLI are known from their shell; any other agent writes its name in the round as `by`, and `record` asks it to until it does.
9. `vantage wait` hands the round over, which the rules allow once every note has an answer and every change since the round before has its account; if one hasn't, it says which. Then it waits for a send that asks for something and prints each note to answer, with its conversation so far, whose each verdict is, and a picture of the page as the person saw it, what the note is on outlined. A note made in the app, taken over, has no picture: `wait` says what the person did there instead. It names the review and where its flows are, for an agent new to it.
10. In the viewer, each person watches, leaves notes, says Looks good or Not quite to each answer and Looks good to each flow, and sends. A note has one state for everyone, the last word on its answer, whoever said it and under their name: Looks good settles it, and a Not quite, even after someone's Looks good, sends it back to the agent. An answer waits for whoever asked for it, by writing the note or sending it back; anyone can judge it. Looks good on a flow, and accepting, are each person's own. Anyone can accept once every flow is good for them and nothing waits for the agent, and `wait` says the review is done.

## Taking a flow over

```bash
vantage take-over <review> "<flow>" ["<step>"]
```

In the viewer it's Take over (T), on the action bar across the frame's bottom edge: while the latest round plays, its flow runs alongside in the background, as far as the playhead has gone and no further, so paused, the moment the playhead rests on is ready a beat later and loaded unseen while you look, and Take over turns the frame itself into the app there, at the same size and place, the time saying Live. It's where you paused, to the action: a recording keeps when each of a step's actions was done, the flow runs as far as the recording had got, and what those actions changed only in the page's memory, like a form opened and typed into, is done again in your browser before it shows, while one that saved something or went to another address isn't done twice; the recording's pointer comes to yours and gives way to it, your name going with it; Taking over is doing: from Take over until you're back, what drives the recording rests: the recording itself, where a click or a pin does nothing; All flows over it, an open card about it (one about the app, a note made there or being tried, still answers), and the row under it (the timeline, which still says Live and grows your fork, speed, size and the foot), stepping back to a third in the app; and the keys with them, so only the app, Note and Back answer, and a note chosen from the bar goes back to the recording at it. Escape, in the app or out of it, lays the recording back over it, the app never seeing it, so what was open there stays open, and T again carries on where you were. A run can't go back, so going back before it starts another. The runs are Vantage's own: each is a window of its own, with its own cookies and storage, in one headless browser Vantage keeps open while it runs, so a new one opens in a moment and nothing is kept waiting; following Kan costs one Chrome, about 350 MB, where a Playwright run of its own for each, with a spare, held about a gigabyte. Recording still runs the flows as Playwright tests. Asked for before it's ready, the recording's pointer says Taking over… and, once a run has got there before, counts down to it, “Taking over in 3…”, a second at a time: each run keeps how long it took from each moment to the next, not counting waiting for the playhead, which on the same app is the same each time to within a few percent, and the viewer adds how long the app took to load in the frame last time; if it isn't there when the count is done, it says Taking over… until it is. A run told where it will be taken over goes straight through the steps before it, keeping nothing to do again there, so a moment deep in Kan's longest flow is ready in about four seconds, not nine. From the command line, it runs the flow to the end of that step (or of the whole flow) on the app as it is now, quickly and unrecorded, from what every flow starts from, then hands its page to you at http://localhost:5830/take-over/1: the same address, cookies and what the app kept in the browser, so you carry on by hand in the real app, from a fresh copy of that moment. The app is passed on through Vantage while the command runs, on every network this machine is on, so a phone on the same one can open it too. On Spliit, with the app already running, it's ready in about three seconds; on Kan too, where the setup signs a new user up through Kan's own sign-in, so the session is Kan's real one, a cookie the page can't read, and it carries over with nothing special for Kan. The page runs on the recordings' clock, so times on it read as they did there. An early try: only the app as it is now, not Before.

## Trying it

```bash
npm install
npx playwright install chromium
npm run build                      # the viewer
cd example
npx vantage start overdue "Show only the overdue invoices"
# write its flows in .vantage/overdue/flows/, then record Before:
npx vantage start overdue
# change the app, then:
npx vantage record overdue
npx vantage wait overdue
npx vantage open overdue           # in another terminal
```

`npm test` holds the review model, the comparison, the command line and what the viewer shows each person to their promises, in about a minute and a half. The comparison's rules are each tested on two small pages, and the command line is run for real in Chromium, as two stories side by side, each in a copy of the example made a git repo. While iterating, `npm run quick` runs everything but those two stories, in about ten seconds; run `npm test` before each commit. `npm run check` checks the types. While working on the viewer, `npm run dev --workspace @mostlyworks/vantage-viewer` serves it with instant reloads beside a running `vantage open`.

Needs Node 24 or later, which runs the TypeScript as it is.
