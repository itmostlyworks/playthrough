# Vantage

The agent says it's done. You decide if it's good.

Vantage records every flow your agent's change touched, before and after, including what the agent didn't mention. Click what's wrong, and the next try lands right beside it. It runs on your machine, with any coding agent that has a shell.

Early work. What's here so far:

- **`packages/review`**: what a review is, and the store that keeps it. A review is rounds the agent records and sends people make, each with one writer. The store takes the loop's steps (start, save a round, take the notes, finish, send, accept, wait) and refuses anything that breaks a rule, saying what's wrong in plain words. It keeps reviews in memory or in a folder. Several people can review, and each person's judgement is their own: what's new to you, which notes wait for you, and whether a flow is good for you are worked out for each person, never kept.
- **`packages/page`**: what's on a page, as a person would name it, and the same thing found again. A reading is the page a step left, everything on it named in Vantage's fixed words; two readings of the same step on two rounds pair thing by thing, which is how the comparison finds what changed, where an answer lands, and where a note sits in every round.
- **`packages/cli`**: the commands an agent runs, over the store, with reviews kept in `.vantage/` in the app's repo. It records each flow, reads the page each step leaves, compares each round with every round before it, places each answer where its note's thing is on the new round, and pictures each note for the agent.
- **`packages/viewer`**: the page a person reviews in, being rebuilt to the prototype's whole experience. Rebuilt so far: the recording and the row under it. A round's flows play like chapters, each opening with its name over its softened first frame; the recording fits, or shows at actual size as a window that follows the pointer or stays where you drag it; side by side shows an earlier round at the same moment, kept in step by the steps both have; the timeline answers the pointer and the drag; and the rounds label rolls like an odometer and opens into a line of rounds to click, drag or swipe through. What's new to you since you last looked at a flow is a diamond on the timeline at its moment, a band over new steps, and a row in the column; stopped on one, what changed is outlined on the recording, and new steps on what they click or type into. The column draws each flow as the recording it is, a picture of its page with its name and length, and under it, by time, its notes and its changes, the flow you're in a line down the column filled to where the playhead is, in two inks only, played and ahead, with every highlight and open card hanging to its right from its number or diamond. A row's header opens and closes it: opened, it goes still to its moment (the frame a change is seen on, or a note was written on), or plays its stretch (new steps, a note on a stretch, or a note whose answer moves, in the step its reply names from a second before the first action that set what it's on moving, nothing stopping it on the way); its time goes there again. A row opens in place into its card: a note's conversation with what the agent changed for it and "Does this fix it?" (Looks good plays on; a saved Not quite stays open, paused, your words in it, until Enter plays on), a change's why in the agent's words, and with several changes at one moment, pointing at one's name outlines just it; held, Compare shows the round you last looked at the flow in place (for an answer, the round its note was written or sent back on), and clicked, side by side. At a flow's end it asks whether it's good: Looks good, which settles the answers in it you haven't judged, or Not quite, a note on the whole flow; once you leave a good flow, its settled rows fold away, and when the column changes its rows glide. The foot names one step from where the playhead is (Play on, Next, Send, Accept), Enter takes it and Ctrl+Enter accepts, and once you've sent it follows the agent; under it a hairline for each flow turns green as it looks good to you, its tick travelling down from the flow. A note you start and leave stays where it was, not saved, and one you remove can be put back for a few seconds. With more than one flow, you arrive on all of them at once, a tile each under the work's name and the round, with Start checking: what needs you large, the rest a quiet row under it, a tile opening close on the first thing new in it (resting there shows how it was) or, resting on it, playing its story. Choosing one flies each picture into its place in the column before the flow plays; All flows over the recording, or Escape, flies them home, and accepting brings you back there as each flow's tick lands in turn. Paused, what a click would note is outlined, named as Vantage names everything (“Settle up button”, “$600.00”, the “Who owes whom” area), and Alt with the wheel widens it to what holds it; a click on empty space is named by what's near it. A note sits on its thing in every round, its pin just outside the outline; playing is watching, so while anything plays, everything drawn over the recording fades (cards, pins, outlines, the line between them, the timeline's words, and the bar, which comes back while the pointer moves over the recording, as a video's controls do), and comes back where it was once it stops, the card kept by its pin; beside it, the earlier round outlines the same thing, for a change too; an open note's answer is outlined in its colour wherever it changed, every place of alike things changed alike, on both rounds; at actual size the view brings what you chose into view; and a note's card greys when what it's on leaves the page. Your note on a change grows out of the change's row: your words under its name, its colour a dot on the diamond, and opened, Compare under your words. On a phone the recording sits at the top as large as the width lets it be, its controls under it, and the column is a drawer from the bottom with the foot at its bottom: pulled up by its handle, it slides over the controls as far as the recording and never over it, and typing in it pulls it up for the keyboard; while there's nothing to press, the foot folds down to its meter. The column's rows fade where they scroll out of view. A tap on the recording notes what's under it, and every flow at once is two to a row.
- **`example`**: a small invoices app to try it on.

## The loop

```bash
vantage start <review> "<title>"   # what was asked, in the person's words
vantage start <review>             # once its flows are written: Before, the app as it is
vantage record <review>            # records every flow, the way a person would use it
vantage wait <review>              # hands the round over, then waits for the notes to answer
vantage open [<review>]            # the viewer, on this machine
```

1. The agent starts a review and writes its flows in `.vantage/<review>/flows/` before it changes anything. They show what was asked, so they're written from the task, not the code: by a fresh subagent given the task and what the screens look like, where the agent can start one. Each flow is one Playwright test for each thing a person does, with a `test.step` for each part of it, importing `test` and `expect` from `@mostlyworks/vantage/test`. The test's name is the flow's. `vantage` alone prints everything an agent needs to know, once (`packages/cli/INSTRUCTIONS.md`); each command then says what's next.
2. `vantage start <review>` again records Before: each flow on the app as it is, as far as the app can take it. What a flow expects of the finished work only waits a moment there, and a flow stops only where the app can't do a step, kept up to that step. A flow the browser stopped, not the app (a page that crashed), fails instead, and running it again tries again. Before is recorded once, and what it was recorded from is kept as a commit, with whatever wasn't committed yet.
3. The agent changes the app. `vantage record` starts it as `vantage/app.json` says (`{ "start": "<command>", "url": "<address>" }`), runs each flow with rrweb in every page, the pointer gliding to what it acts on and a beat after each action, and keeps each recording with where its steps start. Each step ends once the page is at rest, and the page it left is read there as the app shows it, not as the pointer hovers it: everything a person would name, with its box and its look, and a picture. A flow that fails records nothing, and `record` names it and the step it failed at. The fonts, images and stylesheets each page loads are kept with its recording, once each by what's in them, and the viewer plays them from there, so a recording looks the same with the app gone or on another machine; of the sizes an image offers, it shows the one it was recorded at.
4. Every flow starts from the same place each round. Vantage starts it at noon on the day the review started, the clock running on from there, in English and UTC, with the page's random numbers from a fixed seed. The rest is the app's, in `vantage/setup.ts`, written once and committed beside `app.json`: its default export runs before each flow, given the page and Playwright's request, and what it returns is the flow's `setup` (the group it opens, say). A flow is recorded twice the first time it's recorded, on the same app, and what differs between the two changes by itself, like a time the server stamps: `start` or `record` says what it is, and it's left out of every comparison of that flow. Two recordings that don't go the same way (one fails where the other didn't) are refused: the flow doesn't start from the same place each time.
5. A flow added since Before is recorded on Before too, as far as the app as it was can take it. That happens in the agent's own working copy, where its env files and packages are: the work is set aside with git stash, Before is checked out, and the work is put back after, even when recording fails. Stopped halfway, Vantage puts it back before it ends, or the next command does, or it says the two git commands that do. A flow the app as it was can't start has no Before, and isn't tried again.
6. Then `record` compares the round with Before and with every round before it, flow by flow, steps matched by title. A change is a thing and what happened to it, in Vantage's own words, never the agent's: “Export button is new, and moved the Show choice left”, “Status column looks different” where only the pictures differ, “New steps: “Export them”” where a flow takes steps it didn't. The same change in several flows is one change. `record` lists each change since the round before, by its id.
7. The agent says why each of them is there in the round's file, `.vantage/<review>/rounds/<n>/round.json`, under `accounts` by the change's id: its line, the note it was made for, or why nothing really changed. It answers each note there too, where `record` left a reply for each with empty words, placed where the note's thing is on the new recording: found by pairing what was read at the same step, never said by the agent. A note made in the app is on no recording, so its reply isn't placed. A flow that's new to the reviewer can have a `line` at its start: what it starts from, or the choice it shows. Recording again keeps what it wrote.
8. Each round says which agent recorded it. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Gemini CLI are known from their shell; any other agent writes its name in the round as `by`, and `record` asks it to until it does.
9. `vantage wait` hands the round over, which the rules allow once every note has an answer and every change since the round before has its account; if one hasn't, it says which. Then it waits for a send that asks for something and prints each note to answer, with its conversation so far, whose each verdict is, and a picture of the page as the person saw it, what the note is on outlined. A note made in the app, taken over, has no picture: `wait` says what the person did there instead. It names the review and where its flows are, for an agent new to it.
10. In the viewer, each person watches, leaves notes, says Looks good or Not quite to each answer and Looks good to each flow, and sends. A note has one state for everyone, the last word on its answer, whoever said it and under their name: Looks good settles it, and a Not quite, even after someone's Looks good, sends it back to the agent. An answer waits for whoever asked for it, by writing the note or sending it back; anyone can judge it. Looks good on a flow, and accepting, are each person's own. Anyone can accept once every flow is good for them and nothing waits for the agent, and `wait` says the review is done.

## Taking a flow over

```bash
vantage take-over <review> "<flow>" ["<step>"]
```

In the viewer it's Take over (T), at the end of the row under the recording: while the latest round plays, its flow runs alongside in the background, as far as the playhead has gone and no further, so paused, the moment the playhead rests on is ready a beat later and loaded unseen while you look, and Take over turns the frame itself into the app there, at the same size and place, the time saying Live. It's where you paused, to the action: a recording keeps when each of a step's actions was done, the flow runs as far as the recording had got, and what those actions changed only in the page's memory, like a form opened and typed into, is done again in your browser before it shows, while one that saved something or went to another address isn't done twice; the recording's pointer comes to yours and gives way to it, your name going with it; Taking over is doing: from Take over until you're back, what drives the recording rests: the recording itself, where a click or a pin does nothing; All flows over it, an open card about it (one about the app, a note made there or being tried, still answers), the row under it (the timeline, which still says Live and grows your fork, speed, size and the foot) and the column, stepping back to a third in the app; and the keys with them, so only the app, Note and Back answer, and a note chosen from the bar goes back to the recording at it. Escape, in the app or out of it, lays the recording back over it, the app never seeing it, so what was open there stays open, and T again carries on where you were. A run can't go back, so going back before it starts another. The runs are Vantage's own: each is a window of its own, with its own cookies and storage, in one headless browser Vantage keeps open while it runs, so a new one opens in a moment and nothing is kept waiting; following Kan costs one Chrome, about 350 MB, where a Playwright run of its own for each, with a spare, held about a gigabyte. Recording still runs the flows as Playwright tests. Asked for before it's ready, the recording's pointer says Taking over… and, once a run has got there before, counts down to it, “Taking over in 3…”, a second at a time: each run keeps how long it took from each moment to the next, not counting waiting for the playhead, which on the same app is the same each time to within a few percent, and the viewer adds how long the app took to load in the frame last time; if it isn't there when the count is done, it says Taking over… until it is. A run told where it will be taken over goes straight through the steps before it, keeping nothing to do again there, so a moment deep in Kan's longest flow is ready in about four seconds, not nine. From the command line, it runs the flow to the end of that step (or of the whole flow) on the app as it is now, quickly and unrecorded, from what every flow starts from, then hands its page to you at http://localhost:5830/take-over/1: the same address, cookies and what the app kept in the browser, so you carry on by hand in the real app, from a fresh copy of that moment. The app is passed on through Vantage while the command runs, on every network this machine is on, so a phone on the same one can open it too. On Spliit, with the app already running, it's ready in about three seconds; on Kan too, where the setup signs a new user up through Kan's own sign-in, so the session is Kan's real one, a cookie the page can't read, and it carries over with nothing special for Kan. The page runs on the recordings' clock, so times on it read as they did there. An early try: only the app as it is now, not Before.

## Trying it

```bash
npm install
npx playwright install chromium
npm run build                      # the viewer
cd example
npx vantage start overdue "Show only the overdue invoices"
# write its flows in .vantage/overdue/flows/, then record Before:
npx vantage start overdue
# change the app, then:
npx vantage record overdue
npx vantage wait overdue
npx vantage open overdue           # in another terminal
```

`npm test` holds the review model, the comparison, the command line and what the viewer shows each person to their promises, in about a minute and a half. The comparison's rules are each tested on two small pages, and the command line is run for real in Chromium, as two stories side by side, each in a copy of the example made a git repo. While iterating, `npm run quick` runs everything but those two stories, in about ten seconds; run `npm test` before each commit. `npm run check` checks the types. While working on the viewer, `npm run dev --workspace @mostlyworks/vantage-viewer` serves it with instant reloads beside a running `vantage open`.

Needs Node 24 or later, which runs the TypeScript as it is.
